## Direct answer

[China-to-UAE](/guides/china-to-uae/) freight in November 2026 does not default to [Jebel Ali](/ports/jebel-ali/). With the Strait of Hormuz disrupted, most container cargo discharges at [Khor Fakkan](/ports/sharjah/), Fujairah or [Sohar](/ports/sohar/) and moves by truck. Budget 30–50 days door-to-door, 5% duty on CIF plus 5% VAT, and put the land leg in the contract before you book. *(Editorial judgment)*

## What changed in the last 60 days

The UAE freight market is no longer adjusting to one event. It is adjusting to a permanent detour, and the detour is now the product. The dated items below are tracked in our [updates feed](/updates/).

**25 September 2026.** A seven-day proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz was put on the table between Washington and Tehran. It did not become a functioning corridor. Reopening has been discussed repeatedly since spring, and each proposal has hit the same sequencing problem: who guarantees passage, who pays, and who verifies. *(Reported fact; interpretation editorial)*

**5 October 2026.** Oil prices eased as Hormuz traffic rebounded. This is the contradiction that confuses importers: tankers are moving again; container lines are not. The reason is commercial. A tanker is a single asset with a single cargo; a container service is a weekly network with fixed rotations and empty-container flows. Once a carrier has rebuilt around Khor Fakkan, [Salalah](/ports/salalah/) or [Jeddah](/ports/jeddah/), reversing it costs money and takes months. *(Reported fact; interpretation editorial)*

**13–19 October 2026.** The Gemini alliance relaunched its Asia–Middle East loop, but swapped Jebel Ali for Khor Fakkan. The carrier did not cancel the service; it moved the call. Jebel Ali remains a cargo destination, increasingly reached by truck rather than vessel call. *(Reported fact)*

**16 October 2026.** Semafor reported that the UAE's "Zero Hormuz" strategy now runs through Fujairah. It is not a slogan: infrastructure spending is shifting to the east coast, outside the strait, with Fujairah as the pressure-release valve for energy and containerised cargo. *(Reported fact)*

**30 October 2026.** Kuehne+Nagel data showed average waits of 7.25 days at Khor Fakkan and 8.86 at Fujairah over the preceding week; Sharjah was around three days. Maersk imposed temporary booking restrictions on Khor Fakkan, Fujairah and selected Upper Gulf destinations. DP World's CEO warned that rerouting costs would feed through to prices. *(Reported fact)*

**3 November 2026.** The Port of Fujairah resumed oil loadings after an attack, and Linerlytica declared Middle East container congestion a record. The resumption helps energy flows, not container normalisation. *(Reported fact)*

The net effect: the UAE is no longer a single-gateway market. It is a multi-gateway market with a broken centre. Jebel Ali still matters — largest man-made harbour, home of JAFZA, the address on most customs paperwork — but it is no longer where a China-origin container reliably arrives by sea. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Why it matters for your landed cost

**Ocean freight is higher, but not uniformly.** Freightos reported that Shanghai-to-Jebel Ali rates quadrupled from under USD 2,000 to above USD 8,000 per container after the closure began at the end of February 2026. That is a May snapshot, not a current quotation. By the fourth quarter the spread had widened by several thousand dollars depending on discharge port and whether trucking was included. *(Reported fact + editorial estimate)*

**The inland leg is now a first-order cost.** Khor Fakkan to Jebel Ali is roughly 120 kilometres; Fujairah to Jebel Ali about 150. [Salalah](/ports/salalah/) is 1,200+ km away and [Jeddah](/ports/jeddah/) 1,800 through [Saudi Arabia](/countries/saudi-arabia/). Importers comparing only the ocean quote choose the wrong port. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Time has become the expensive variable.** Pre-war, Shanghai-to-Jebel Ali FCL was commonly quoted at 18–24 days port-to-port, Shenzhen at 14–20. Those numbers are still repeated on forwarder websites, which makes quote comparisons misleading. Now the ocean leg to an east-coast port plus the 12–15 days needed to transfer a container to Jebel Ali, Sharjah or Abu Dhabi produces a 30–50 day door-to-door cycle for FCL; LCL adds 7–10 days. *(Industry claim; the 12–15 day transfer is reported by Kuehne+Nagel via Splash247)*

For a distributor carrying six weeks of inventory, that is a working-capital problem; for an [e-commerce](/sectors/ecommerce/) seller, a stock-out problem; for project cargo, a demurrage problem. *(Editorial judgment)*

## How to act: a routing decision in five steps

**1. Choose the discharge port by consignee location, not habit.** Dubai and JAFZA cargo should default to Khor Fakkan plus truck. Abu Dhabi should test [Khalifa](/ports/khalifa/) first, then Khor Fakkan. Northern Emirates should test [Sharjah's Khalid Port](/ports/sharjah/), accepting more schedule risk inside Hormuz. [Saudi-bound](/countries/saudi-arabia/) cargo should not automatically transload in the UAE; [Jeddah](/ports/jeddah/) or [Dammam](/ports/dammam/) direct is often cheaper once border and inland costs are counted. *(Editorial judgment)*

**2. Insist on a through bill of lading with a named land leg.** The common failure is a carrier B/L to Khor Fakkan with no contractual owner of the trucking leg. The box arrives, the forwarder's quote turns out to end at the port gate, and it sits in a yard while three parties argue. A through B/L, or a written addendum naming the trucker, transit time and free time, is the minimum. *(Industry claim + editorial judgment)*

**3. Pre-clear before the vessel arrives.** [Customs clearance](/guides/customs-clearance/) is fast when the file is clean — often two to five working days — and slow when it is not. The difference is documentation: missing certificate of origin, mismatched HS code, a trade licence that does not cover the activity, or a regulated product without [ECAS](/guides/certification/). Start the file at loading, not at arrival. *(Industry claim)*

**4. Model duty and VAT on CIF, not the supplier invoice.** Duty is generally 5% of CIF. Import VAT is 5% of customs value plus duty plus excise, so the cash outlay at clearance is roughly 10.25% of CIF for a standard product, before anti-dumping. [Car batteries](/sectors/batteries/), ceramic tile and hydraulic cement can face anti-dumping up to 67.5% of CIF; tobacco and energy drinks face 100% excise. *(Reported fact)*

**5. Build a buffer and a fallback into the contract.** Plan 45–55 days factory-to-warehouse for [FCL](/guides/fcl/) and 55–70 for [LCL](/guides/lcl/). Keep a second routing option open — Sohar for northern UAE, Khalifa for Abu Dhabi, Jeddah for Saudi-bound — and agree who pays if the carrier switches discharge port. Do not accept a fixed-delivery penalty without a force-majeure and routing-change carve-out. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Cost and transit drivers

This is a driver list, not a rate card. A single "China to UAE" number in this market is either a guess or a route that no longer exists.

| Driver | What it does to your cost | What to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Ocean base rate | Sets the floor; currently volatile by carrier and discharge port | Is the quote to Jebel Ali direct, or to Khor Fakkan with a land leg? |
| War-risk and emergency surcharges | Freightos reported USD 200–500 per container across lanes after the closure | Which surcharges are pass-through, and which are capped? |
| Discharge port choice | Khor Fakkan/Fujairah add trucking; Salalah/Jeddah add long-haul domestic legs | Who owns the container between port and door? |
| Port waiting time | 7.25 days at Khor Fakkan, 8.86 at Fujairah, about 3 at Sharjah on 30 October | What is the current vessel wait, not last month's? |
| Customs and certification | Clean file: 2–5 working days. Missing ECAS or wrong HS code: 1–3 weeks | Who checks the certificate before shipment? |
| Duty and VAT | 5% duty + 5% VAT on CIF and duty; excise and anti-dumping for specific goods | What is the HS code, and is it subject to anti-dumping? |
| Demurrage and detention | Can exceed the ocean freight on a stalled box | How many free days at destination, in writing? |

**Transit benchmarks.** Pre-war port-to-port times were commonly quoted as Shanghai 18–24 days, Ningbo 18–24, Shenzhen 14–20, Guangzhou 16–22, Qingdao 22–28. Treat those as historical. Now add east-coast discharge, the 12–15 day port-to-door transfer, and truck variability: a realistic FCL door-to-door range is 30–50 days, LCL 55–70. [Air freight](/guides/air-freight/) is 4–7 days for priority cargo, with tight capacity and rates well above 2025. *(Industry claim)*

## Ports and routing: the new UAE gateway map

**Khor Fakkan (Sharjah, east coast).** The main container valve. It sits on the Gulf of Oman, outside Hormuz, in Sharjah's east-coast exclave. It is handling ships up to 24,000 TEU on MSC's expanded Far East–Middle East service, with average waits of 7.25 days. The terminal is doubling capacity, but not before the current congestion peak. Expect 12–15 days from discharge to Jebel Ali, Sharjah or Abu Dhabi — three times the pre-war five-day transfer. *(Reported fact)*

**Fujairah (east coast).** The pressure-release valve for energy and a growing container option. It is outside Hormuz, but its container infrastructure is thinner than Khor Fakkan's, and waits reached 8.86 days in late October. It is the centre of the UAE's "Zero Hormuz" strategy. For containers it is a secondary choice unless the consignee is in the northern emirates or the cargo is project or breakbulk. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

**Sharjah (Khalid Port and Hamriyah).** Inside Hormuz, close to Sharjah's industrial zones, with about three-day waits in late October. A useful fallback for northern-emirates cargo, but it carries more schedule risk because vessels still transit the strait. *(Reported fact)*

**Jebel Ali (Dubai).** The largest man-made harbour in the world: 67 berths, 134.68 square kilometres, and 80+ weekly services to 150+ ports in normal times. In November 2026 it is largely restricted to ad hoc vessel calls. Cargo still arrives, by landbridge from Khor Fakkan or Fujairah. *(Reported fact)*

**Khalifa Port (Abu Dhabi).** The deep-water alternative for Abu Dhabi and western UAE cargo, with rail and road links. It sits inside Hormuz, so shares the strait risk, but is less congested than the east-coast terminals and is the natural first test for Abu Dhabi-bound industrial cargo. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Oman: Sohar and Salalah.** Sohar is the practical overland alternative for northern UAE: roughly 180–220 kilometres to Dubai, with waits of two to three days in late October. Salalah is a deep-water transshipment hub at 84% yard density with delays up to 36 hours; its overland leg to the UAE is over 1,200 kilometres, so it is a transshipment option, not a trucking one. *(Reported fact)*

**Jeddah (Saudi Arabia, Red Sea).** Viable for Saudi-bound cargo and, in extremis, for UAE-bound cargo trucked across Saudi Arabia. Jeddah's yards were at 76% density in late October. The overland leg to Dubai is roughly 1,800 kilometres with Saudi transit customs; it is not a casual substitute for a Gulf port. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

## Free zone versus mainland: where duty and VAT actually land

UAE free zones are not tax-free shops. They are duty- and VAT-deferral machines, and the deferral ends when goods enter the mainland.

**JAFZA** is the largest customs bonded zone in the Middle East: 11,000+ businesses from 150+ countries, 23.9% of Dubai's FDI. Goods entering JAFZA are not subject to UAE duty or import VAT while they stay in the zone. That is why it works for re-export, regional distribution and e-commerce fulfilment. *(Reported fact)*

**The mainland transfer is the taxable event.** When goods leave JAFZA for a UAE mainland customer, an import declaration is filed, 5% duty is payable on CIF, and 5% VAT is payable on the customs value plus duty. If the goods are re-exported outside the UAE, duty and VAT are generally not triggered — but the destination country's rules apply, and Saudi Arabia, for example, requires SABER compliance regardless of the UAE origin. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

**DMCC, DAFZA, SAIF Zone and RAKEZ** run similar bonded regimes. DAFZA suits air-sea e-commerce, DMCC commodities and trading, SAIF Zone and RAKEZ light manufacturing and cost-sensitive warehousing. The customs logic is the same: suspension inside, declaration and payment on release to the mainland. *(Industry claim)*

**Corporate tax is a separate question.** The headline rate is 9%, with 0% on the first AED 375,000 of taxable income. Qualifying free-zone income can be taxed at 0%, but only if the entity meets the substance and qualifying-income conditions. A free-zone licence is not, by itself, a corporate tax exemption. Confirm the position before signing the lease. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

## Customs and duty: the 5% + 5% stack

The UAE is part of the GCC Customs Union. For most non-GCC goods, duty is 5% of CIF. Some goods are duty-free; alcohol and tobacco are higher; anti-dumping duties apply to specific products, including car batteries, ceramic and porcelain tiles, and hydraulic cement, reaching 67.5% of CIF. *(Reported fact)*

Import VAT is 5%. The base is not the supplier invoice. It is the customs value plus customs duty plus any excise. For a standard product with 5% duty and 5% VAT, the cash payable at clearance is about 10.25% of CIF. For a product with 100% excise — tobacco, energy drinks, electronic smoking devices — the VAT base rises accordingly. *(Reported fact)*

Documents decide whether clearance takes days or weeks:

- Commercial invoice with correct HS code, unit value, currency and Incoterm; packing list with weights, dimensions and carton counts.
- Bill of lading or sea waybill with the consignee matching the trade licence; certificate of origin where a preferential rate is claimed.
- UAE trade licence and customs client code for the importer of record.
- Product certificates: ECAS for regulated electrical goods, toys, cosmetics and construction materials; TDRA for telecom devices; Dubai Municipality food registration; MOHAP for medical devices.
- MSDS and UN 38.3 test reports for batteries and dangerous goods.

*(Industry claim, compiled from UAE import practice)*

**The importer of record is the choke point.** A UAE trade licence is required, and the licence activity must cover the goods. Sellers who use a forwarder's [DDP](/guides/ddp/) service without a named importer are relying on the forwarder's licence, which is legal only if the forwarder is genuinely acting as importer and is registered for VAT. If the arrangement is a workaround, the risk lands on the seller when customs audits the valuation or the consignee. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Valuation is the second choke point.** UAE customs can reject a declared value below market, with fines and delays. Related-party transactions, samples and "gift" declarations are common triggers. Keep the invoice, payment proof and a price benchmark for the HS code. *(Industry claim)*

## Product certification: what stops clearance

The UAE has several product-compliance doors; the wrong one produces a detention notice. **ECAS** covers regulated electrical equipment, [electronics](/sectors/electronics/), toys, construction materials and food-contact materials. **TDRA** covers telecom and radio devices. **Dubai Municipality** regulates food and cosmetics (Montaji). **MOHAP** regulates medical devices and pharmaceuticals.

Batteries are the most underestimated category. Lithium cells and packs need UN 38.3 test reports, and products containing them need a dangerous-goods declaration. A container of power banks without UN 38.3 is not a customs delay; it is a refusal to load or a seizure at destination. Certify before you book, not after. *(Industry claim + editorial judgment)*

## E-commerce: Amazon FBA and noon in a disrupted market

The UAE remains one of the easiest GCC markets for cross-border e-commerce, but 2026 has made "easy" conditional on inventory planning. Amazon.ae FBA and noon require a UAE importer of record, a licence covering the product category, and compliant labelling. Sellers typically ship to a JAFZA or Dubai industrial 3PL, then replenish fulfilment centres in smaller batches. FBA inbound appointments, carton labelling and FNSKU rules are strict, and a rejected shipment can sit for weeks.

Routing matters more than platform. With Jebel Ali restricted and Khor Fakkan congested, a 30-day replenishment cycle is now 45–55 days. Hold 8–10 weeks of cover on top SKUs, split inventory between a JAFZA 3PL and the marketplace hub, reserve air for launches and stock-out rescue, and confirm the importer-of-record arrangement in writing. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Risk and compliance

**War-risk insurance.** Standard marine cargo policies exclude war, strikes and terrorism unless endorsed. Gulf war-risk cover is available but priced separately, sometimes with voyage-specific conditions. Check that the policy covers the actual discharge port and the overland leg, not just the ocean segment. A policy ending at Khor Fakkan does not cover a truck to Jebel Ali. *(Industry claim)*

**Sanctions and re-export controls.** The UAE suspended trade with Iran in August 2026 after an antiship missile strike. Goods with Iranian origin, ownership or end-use should not transit the UAE without legal review. Re-export from JAFZA to Iran is a compliance red line. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

**Force majeure and contract terms.** Carriers have invoked force majeure, changed discharge ports and imposed congestion surcharges. Your sales contract should say which Incoterm applies, which port is the agreed discharge point, who bears the cost of a carrier-initiated diversion, and what happens if the cargo cannot be delivered within a stated period. "CIF Jebel Ali" is ambiguous when the carrier discharges at Khor Fakkan. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Demurrage, detention and abandonment.** A box stuck at Khor Fakkan accrues demurrage; at a 3PL, storage; if nobody clears it, the port authority can sell it. All three usually trace to one missing document nobody checked before sailing. *(Industry claim)*

**Counterparty risk.** The dirham is pegged to the dollar, so FX is not the issue. A new UAE buyer with a free-zone licence and a PO box is not automatically creditworthy. Use a letter of credit or staged payment for first orders. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Our read

**Do not treat Jebel Ali as the default.** A direct Jebel Ali B/L in November 2026 is a promise the carrier may not keep — see our [direct-versus-landbridge comparison](/compare/jebel-ali-vs-direct/). Book to Khor Fakkan or Sohar, price the truck, and treat Jebel Ali as a destination, not a discharge port. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Do use JAFZA for inventory, but not on a long lease.** The duty and VAT deferral is valuable and the 3PL ecosystem is deep, but a five-year lease assumes Jebel Ali direct calls return on schedule. Start with flexible 3PL space and a one-year licence. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Do not ship low-margin bulky goods by air.** Air freight is for high-value, time-critical or launch inventory. Using it to patch a routine replenishment gap destroys the unit economics of furniture, building materials, bulky consumer goods and low-value electronics. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Do consider Saudi direct if your end market is Saudi.** A Jebel Ali-to-Riyadh truck is roughly 1,000 kilometres plus border formalities; our [UAE-versus-Saudi comparison](/compare/uae-vs-saudi/) sets out the trade-off. Dammam or Jeddah direct is often cheaper and simpler, especially for project and heavy cargo. *(Editorial judgment)*

**What would change our mind.** Two conditions: weekly liner calls at Jebel Ali for eight consecutive weeks, and Khor Fakkan waits below three days. Until both are true, multi-gateway routing is safer. *(Editorial judgment)*

## FAQ

**Is Jebel Ali Port open?**
Yes, but not as a normal container gateway. It handles ad hoc vessel calls, and cargo reaches Jebel Ali by landbridge from Khor Fakkan and Fujairah. Direct liner services have largely moved to Khor Fakkan. Check the specific service with the carrier. *(Reported fact)*

**How long does China-to-UAE shipping take in November 2026?**
Plan 30–50 days door-to-door for FCL and 55–70 for LCL. The ocean leg is not the bottleneck; discharge port, the 12–15 day port-to-door transfer and truck availability are. Air is 4–7 days for priority cargo. *(Industry claim)*

**How much is UAE customs duty?**
Generally 5% of CIF. Some goods are duty-free, alcohol and tobacco are higher, and anti-dumping can reach 67.5% on car batteries, ceramic tiles and hydraulic cement. Check the HS code before pricing landed cost. *(Reported fact)*

**Can I import into a UAE free zone and avoid duty and VAT?**
You can defer them. Goods in JAFZA or another bonded zone are not subject to duty or import VAT while they stay in the zone. On release to the mainland, both become payable. Re-export generally avoids UAE duty and VAT, but destination rules still apply. *(Reported fact)*

**Do I need a UAE company to import?**
Yes, for a normal commercial import: a UAE trade licence, a customs client code and a licence activity covering the goods. If you use a forwarder's importer-of-record service, confirm in writing who is the legal importer, who pays duty and VAT, and who is liable if valuation is challenged. *(Industry claim)*

**Is air freight a viable alternative?**
For high-value, urgent or launch cargo, yes. For routine replenishment of bulky or low-margin goods, no — the [sea-versus-air trade-off](/compare/sea-vs-air/) rarely favours air at scale. Capacity into Dubai is tight, rates are elevated, and the advantage shrinks once customs and last-mile delivery are included. *(Editorial judgment)*
