## Direct answer

Air freight from China to the GCC in November 2026 costs roughly USD 4.50–8.50 per kilo to Dubai for 100–500 kg shipments, and USD 5.50–10.50 to Riyadh, Jeddah, Doha, Kuwait or Bahrain. Door-to-door transit is 3–6 days, or 5–8 days in a peak. The rate is not the price: air bills on chargeable weight, so a light carton can cost twice what its actual weight suggests. Capacity is the other constraint — Middle East and South Asia belly space is still tight, and a booking made three days before cut-off is a booking at the spot price. *(Editorial judgment)*

## What changed in the last 60 days

**The Hormuz closure made air the escape valve, then the bottleneck.** With container routing through [Khor Fakkan](/ports/sharjah/) and [Jeddah](/ports/jeddah/) unreliable, urgent cargo moved to air: spare parts, launch inventory, medical goods and e-commerce replenishment. Chinese trade press reported air rates at near three-year highs in 2026, with some Middle East lanes doubling versus March. The surge was not only demand; it was also a capacity problem, because passenger belly space to the Gulf had not fully recovered. The lane changes are logged in our [update feed](/updates/). *(Reported fact)*

**Capacity stayed tight into the fourth quarter.** WorldACD's week 43 report (late October 2026) described Middle East and South Asia capacity as still constrained, with global capacity down about 1% week on week. For shippers, that means the old habit of booking three days before cut-off no longer works on Gulf lanes; the space is allocated to contract customers and e-commerce consolidators first. *(Reported fact)*

**Dubai's two-airport system became a routing decision.** Emirates SkyCargo has operated DXB and DWC as a dual hub for a decade, and the split matters more in 2026: DXB carries passenger belly plus cargo, DWC carries dedicated freighters and sits next to Jebel Ali. With [Jebel Ali](/ports/jebel-ali/)'s container volumes down 90.1% in Q2, DWC's freighter role and its road link to the JAFZA free zone became more valuable, not less. *(Reported fact)*

**War-risk and fuel surcharges became permanent lines.** Oman Air Cargo added war-risk and fuel surcharges on a per-kilo basis in March 2026. Cathay Cargo's Hong Kong export fuel surcharge for 1–15 November 2026 is HK$7.1/kg on medium-haul routes. UPS publishes a monthly international air-freight fuel matrix with per-kilo surcharges by destination region. These are not temporary add-ons; they are now part of the rate structure. *(Reported fact)*

**Saudi and UAE customs tightened document checks.** SABER shipment certificates must be valid before arrival, and a certificate issued but unused for 60 days can expire. In the UAE, ECAS and TDRA registrations are checked against the AWB and invoice. Air cargo clears faster than sea, but it also fails faster: a missing certificate on a 300 kg shipment is a 300 kg shipment sitting in a bonded warehouse at USD 1–3/kg/day. *(Industry claim)*

## Why it matters: air is a different product, not faster sea freight

Sea freight sells a container; air freight sells **chargeable weight and a cut-off**. The aircraft has a fixed volume and a fixed payload, and the airline prices the binding constraint. That is why air economics invert [sea freight](/guides/fcl/) economics: on the water you try to fill a box; in the air you try to make cargo dense enough to pay for its space. *(Editorial judgment)*

Three consequences follow.

**The volumetric divisor is 6,000.** Air chargeable weight is calculated as length × width × height in centimetres divided by 6,000, compared with actual weight, and the greater number is billed. One cubic metre is 166.7 kg. A carton that is 60 × 50 × 40 cm is 0.12 cbm and 20 kg actual, but 20 kg volumetric — at the divisor it bills 20 kg. Increase the height to 60 cm and it becomes 0.18 cbm and 30 kg volumetric, a 50% increase in billed weight for the same product. *(Industry claim)*

**Density is the product design.** High-value, dense cargo — electronics, spare parts, cosmetics, pharmaceuticals — pays air and still works. Bulky, low-value cargo does not, unless the buyer pays for speed. The practical test is value per kilo: below about USD 8–12/kg, air rarely survives a landed-cost comparison. *(Editorial judgment)*

**The cut-off is a hard wall.** An air booking has a cut-off measured in hours, a screening deadline and a ULD build sequence. Cargo that misses it flies when there is space — in a tight market, several days later. *(Industry claim)*

## Chargeable weight: the five ways your invoice grows

**1. The carton is bigger than the product.** Suppliers quote product dimensions; the airline measures the shipping carton. A 5% increase in each dimension is a 16% increase in volume and, for light cargo, a 16% increase in billed weight.

**2. Pallets are measured with overhang.** A 120 × 100 × 160 cm pallet is 1.92 cbm and 320 kg volumetric at the 6,000 divisor. Cartons overhanging 5 cm per side add about 0.15 cbm and 25 kg — paid on light cargo.


**4. The minimum charge applies.** A 45 kg shipment may be billed at the 45 kg rate, a minimum charge, or the next weight break — whichever is higher. On small shipments, the minimum is often the whole invoice.

**5. Weight breaks change the rate.** Air rates are tiered: 45 kg, 100 kg, 300 kg, 500 kg, 1,000 kg. A 95 kg shipment can be cheaper if declared at 100 kg, because the 100 kg rate is lower. This is legitimate, not a trick, but it requires the forwarder to check the break before quoting. *(Industry claim)*

The practical response is to ask for the **chargeable weight calculation** with the quote: dimensions used, divisor, actual weight, volumetric weight and the weight break applied. A quote that gives a per-kilo rate without a chargeable weight is not a quote; it is a rate indication. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Airport pairs: choose the door, not the hub

**Shanghai (PVG) to Dubai (DXB).** The broadest cargo capacity from China, with passenger belly and freighter options. Good for machinery, electronics and mixed pallets; the [China–UAE route guide](/guides/china-to-uae/) covers the sea alternative. DXB clearance is fast when the file is clean, but the airport is passenger-first; freighter cargo often lands at DWC instead. *(Industry claim)*

**Guangzhou (CAN) and Shenzhen (SZX) to Dubai.** The e-commerce corridor: CAN has heavy belly capacity, SZX sits next to electronics manufacturers and has direct freighters. It is the natural pair for FBA or noon replenishment, but screening and DG rules are strict because the lanes carry consumer electronics and batteries. *(Industry claim)*

**Hong Kong (HKG) to Dubai, Riyadh, Doha.** The premium option: more wide-body capacity and more frequent departures at higher rates. HKG suits high-value goods, samples and cargo that must fly on a specific day, and it is the origin most exposed to fuel-surcharge changes. *(Industry claim)*

**China to Jeddah (JED) and Riyadh (RUH).** Saudia Cargo's hubs are Jeddah and Riyadh, with freighter and belly capacity from China. JED serves western Saudi and the Red Sea corridor; RUH serves the central region and connects to [Riyadh Dry Port](/ports/riyadh-dry-port/). The [China–Saudi route guide](/guides/china-to-saudi-arabia/) covers the sea option. Dammam (DMM) has less wide-body capacity and is usually served via Riyadh or Dubai plus truck. *(Reported fact)*

**China to Doha (DOH), Kuwait (KWI), Bahrain (BAH), Muscat (MCT).** Smaller markets, fewer direct frequencies. Qatar Airways added routes through 2026, which helps [Hamad](/ports/hamad/); Kuwait and Bahrain rely on connections via Dubai or Doha. Expect 1–3 extra days and a higher rate per kilo. *(Industry claim)*

**DXB versus DWC.** DXB is the passenger hub with belly cargo and a large cargo terminal; DWC is the freighter airport next to Jebel Ali, with dedicated cargo handling and road access to the JAFZA industrial area. If your consignee is in Jebel Ali or Dubai South, DWC can save a truck leg and a day. If the cargo arrives on a passenger flight, it lands at DXB and the truck is unavoidable. Ask which airport, not just "Dubai". *(Editorial judgment)*

## How to act: the air booking sequence

**1. Book capacity before you book the cargo.** On Gulf lanes in late 2026, space is the constraint. Get a named flight, a cutoff time and a rate validity date. A quote "subject to space" is not a booking. *(Editorial judgment)*

**2. Confirm the chargeable weight and the weight break.** Ask for dimensions, divisor, actual and volumetric weight, and the break applied. If the cargo is near a break, ask whether declaring up is cheaper. *(Industry claim)*

**3. Pre-alert the destination agent.** Send the invoice, packing list, AWB draft, certificate numbers and the importer's customs code before departure. Air cargo clears in hours when the file is ready and in days when it is not. The [customs clearance guide](/guides/customs-clearance/) covers the document set. *(Editorial judgment)*

**4. Screen and declare dangerous goods properly.** Batteries, aerosols, magnets, chemicals and some machinery need a DG declaration, an SDS and often a cargo-only aircraft. The [batteries sector page](/sectors/batteries/) covers the product side; the [certification guide](/guides/certification/) covers SABER and ECAS. *(Industry claim)*

**5. Arrange delivery and an appointment before arrival.** Airport storage is free for a short window — often 24–72 hours — and then charged per kilo per day. A consignee without a truck and a receiving slot turns a three-day air shipment into a six-day one. *(Industry claim)*

**6. Insure the shipment and keep the AWB clean.** Air liability is capped by the Montreal Convention, roughly 22 SDR per kilo unless a higher value is declared. For high-value electronics, declare the value and buy all-risk cover. *(Industry claim)*

Two structural notes. **Air rates are quoted per kilo but priced per flight**: airlines sell capacity in blocks and forwarders resell an allocation, so when a block is full the next kilo is spot — which is why two forwarders can quote USD 5.20 and USD 7.80 on the same day. **Surcharges are where the margin moves**: a base rate held for 30 days with floating fuel and war-risk is not a fixed price, and a quote that hides the mechanism is not comparable. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Air for e-commerce: launch inventory, not container economics

Air is the standard first shipment for Amazon.ae FBA, noon and direct-to-consumer launches in the GCC. The logic is not freight cost but speed to first sale: a 300 kg air shipment can be in a Dubai fulfilment centre in five days while the sea container waits for a Khor Fakkan slot. For a new SKU with unknown demand, that speed is worth more than the freight difference. *(Editorial judgment)*

Three specifics matter. **Labels and appointments**: FBA inbound needs carton labels, FNSKU labels and an appointment, and a rejected shipment can sit for weeks. **Importer of record**: Amazon is not the importer; the seller or a 3PL needs a UAE trade licence and customs code. **Batteries**: consumer electronics are regulated, and a mis-declared battery can stop the shipment. The [e-commerce sector page](/sectors/ecommerce/) covers the fulfilment model. *(Industry claim)*

For replenishment, the cheaper pattern is sea for the bulk and air for the gap. That is the [sea versus air comparison](/compare/sea-vs-air/) in practice: air is not a substitute for sea, it is the insurance policy on a sea plan. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Dangerous goods and batteries: the rules that stop shipments

Lithium batteries are the most common DG problem on China–GCC air lanes. The framework is the IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations, and the practical thresholds are well known: cells and batteries under 100 Wh can often ship as non-restricted cargo under specific packing instructions; 100–160 Wh require a DG declaration and, for UN3480/UN3090, cargo-only aircraft; above 160 Wh is a specialist shipment. UN3481 (contained in equipment) and UN3091 (packed with equipment) have different rules from UN3480/UN3090 (standalone). *(Industry claim)*

Four failure modes we see:

- **The supplier ships a "battery" as a "device".** A power bank is UN3480, not UN3481, and the packing instruction changes.
- **The SDS is generic.** Airlines want a battery test summary and a UN38.3 report that matches the cell.
- **The state variation is ignored.** Some Gulf states restrict or require pre-approval for specific battery classes; the airline's DG desk is the authority, not the forwarder's salesperson.
- **The cargo is consolidated with incompatible goods.** A DG consignment cannot share a ULD with general cargo unless the segregation rules allow it.

A mis-declared DG shipment is not a rate negotiation. It is refused at acceptance, offloaded, or held at destination, and the shipper bears the cost. For a product launch, that is worse than a higher freight rate. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Local detail: five air gateways

**Dubai (DXB and DWC).** The region's air hub and its most competitive cargo market. DXB handles belly and cargo; DWC handles freighters and sits beside Jebel Ali. Dubai Customs' Mirsal 2 system processes declarations electronically, and the free-zone options (JAFZA, DAFZA) allow bonded storage and re-export — the [UAE guide](/countries/uae/) covers the licence and importer requirements. Clearance is usually 1–2 working days when the file is clean; storage after free time is charged per kilo per day. *(Industry claim)*

**Jeddah (JED) and Riyadh (RUH).** Saudia Cargo's two hubs. JED is the Red Sea door and the busiest Saudi cargo airport; RUH is the central door and connects to Riyadh Dry Port. Saudi customs runs through Fasah, and SABER certificates are checked electronically. A missing shipment certificate can hold cargo at the airport even when the flight was on time. *(Industry claim)*

**Doha (DOH).** Qatar Airways Cargo has expanded capacity through 2026, and Hamad International's cargo terminal is efficient. Doha is a good connection point for Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman, but a direct China–Doha freighter is less frequent than China–Dubai. *(Industry claim)*

**Kuwait (KWI) and Bahrain (BAH).** Smaller cargo markets with limited direct wide-body capacity. Most freight connects through Dubai or Doha, adding a day and a handling fee. Both are expensive per kilo relative to Dubai because the last leg is a narrow-body or a truck. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Risk and compliance: what air does not forgive

**Screening and security.** Air cargo is screened before loading; a shipment that fails screening is held, and the shipper pays storage. Known consignor status can speed this up, but it requires an audit and is not available to one-off shippers. *(Industry claim)*

**Sanctions and export controls.** Airlines screen the shipper, consignee and commodity. A dual-use item — certain electronics, drones, machine tools — can require an export licence from China and an import licence in the GCC. The UAE's suspension of trade with Iran in August 2026 makes any Iranian nexus a hard stop. *(Reported fact)*

**Valuation and duty.** Air cargo is not a way to avoid duty. GCC customs assesses duty on CIF value, and air freight is part of CIF. A low invoice with a high air freight bill invites a valuation query. For Saudi Arabia, VAT is 15% on the duty-inclusive value; for the UAE, 5%. *(Industry claim)*

**Temperature and handling.** Pharmaceuticals, cosmetics and some food products need a cold chain. A passive shipper with gel packs is not a validated cold chain; if the product requires 2–8°C, ask for a temperature logger and a carrier with a pharma product. *(Industry claim)*

**Abandonment and storage.** Airport storage is expensive and finite. If the consignee does not clear, the shipment can be auctioned or destroyed after a defined period, with the shipper liable for charges. Air abandonment happens faster than sea abandonment because the storage clock is shorter. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Our read

**Do** use air for launch inventory, spare parts and high-value electronics where a week of delay costs more than USD 3–6 per kilo. That is the real test, not the freight rate.

**Do** ask for chargeable weight and the weight break with every quote. A per-kilo rate without a chargeable weight is a number, not a price.

**Do** book capacity early on Gulf lanes. In a constrained market, the difference between a booking and a quote is a week.

**Do** route by consignee, not by habit: DWC for Jebel Ali and Dubai South, DXB for Dubai city, RUH for central Saudi, JED for the west, DOH for Qatar and connections.

**Avoid** air for furniture, packaging and anything worth less than about USD 8–12 per kilo; the landed cost rarely survives.

**Avoid** consolidating batteries with general cargo unless the DG desk has approved the build. The cost of a refusal is higher than the saving.

**Avoid** treating a DDP air quote as a fixed price. Duty, VAT and destination charges are estimates unless the quote names the HS band and the importer of record. The [DDP guide](/guides/ddp/) explains why.

## FAQ

**How much does air freight from China to Dubai cost?**
About USD 4.50–8.50 per kilo for 100–500 kg in November 2026, before surcharges and destination charges. Smaller shipments pay more per kilo because of minimum charges; 1,000 kg shipments can fall below USD 4.50/kg on competitive lanes.

**How long does air freight take door to door?**
Three to six days typically: one to three days flying, one to two days clearance, one to two days delivery. Peak season and missing certificates add three to seven days.

**Why is my chargeable weight higher than the actual weight?**
Because air bills the greater of actual and volumetric weight, at 1 cbm = 167 kg. Light, bulky cartons bill on volume. Ask for the dimensions and divisor used; a 1 cm difference per side can add 5–10%.

**Can I ship lithium batteries by air to the GCC?**
Yes, with the right classification, UN38.3 report, SDS and DG declaration. Standalone lithium-ion batteries (UN3480) above 100 Wh usually require cargo-only aircraft. Some carriers and states require pre-approval; check with the airline's DG desk before booking.

**Do I pay VAT and duty on air cargo?**
Yes. Duty is assessed on CIF value, which includes air freight; VAT is charged on the duty-inclusive value — 15% in Saudi Arabia, 5% in the UAE and Oman, 10% in Bahrain, 0% in Qatar and Kuwait.
