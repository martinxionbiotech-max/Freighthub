## Direct answer

Riyadh Dry Port is Saudi Arabia's largest inland container terminal, in Al-Malaz/As-Sinaiyah on Exit 16, connected to [King Abdulaziz Port in Dammam](/ports/dammam/) by up to seven daily trains. It covers 918,639 m², handles more than a million TEU a year across the wider ecosystem, and since April 2026 offers 30 days of free storage for goods and transit containers. Use it to clear Riyadh-bound cargo inland, to hold stock closer to the customer and to avoid coastal storage bills. Do not treat the 30-day window as free clearance: duty, VAT, inspection and carrier demurrage run on separate clocks.

The dry port is a tool for importers with a rail slot, a Saudi bond and a clean SABER file. Without those three, it is a warehouse with a customs desk and a long truck ride back to the coast. *(Editorial judgment)*

## What changed in the last 60 days

**23 April 2026 — free storage at Riyadh Dry Port rose from 7 to 30 days.** Saudi Arabia Railways extended the free period for various goods and transit containers, explicitly linking the change to its five new logistics corridors. The practical effect is a month of breathing room for importers whose documents arrive after the container. The strategic effect is to make the inland node more competitive with coastal storage, where free time is shorter and daily charges compound faster. *(Reported fact)*

**April 2026 — SAR launched five new freight corridors.** The network links eastern ports with western ports, northern border crossings and regional markets. Riyadh Dry Port is the pivot. A corridor is not a train; it is a commercial product with a schedule, a tariff and a customs procedure. The five-corridor launch is the first time SAR has marketed the network as a single product rather than a set of rail segments. *(Reported fact)*

**1 March 2026 — a new Riyadh Dry Port tariff took effect.** SGP Intermodal published the 1/2026 tariff on behalf of SAR, covering shipping-line charges, broker and consignee charges, and rail haulage. The tariff keeps a 20% surcharge for hazardous cargo, sets overweight limits at 25 t for a 20 ft and 30.5 t for a 40 ft, and prices X-ray, reefer plugging and re-stuffing as separate services. The direction is toward transparent line items and away from all-in quotes. *(Reported fact)*

**December 2025 — SGP secured the concession to develop and run the Riyadh Dry Port Ecosystem.** The agreement with SAR targets 1.5 million containers a year by 2030, with a modernisation programme covering civil infrastructure, new equipment, automation and a unified digital platform. SGP had operated the dry port since 2021; the new concession turns it into a long-term integrated operator across the sea terminal, the rail link and the inland node. *(Reported fact)*

**2026 — rail volumes kept rising.** Saudi rail carried about 47,000 TEU in the second quarter of 2026 across the network, and the East Train alone moved about 887,900 standard containers in 2024, up 27% on 2023. The absolute numbers are small against the Kingdom's 8.3 million TEU of port throughput, but the direction is clear: rail is taking share in the Dammam–Riyadh corridor. *(Reported fact)*

**October 2026 — SGP launched a new terminal operating system at Dammam.** The TOS gives the operator a single view of vessel, yard and rail planning. For Riyadh importers, the benefit is earlier visibility of a late rail connection or a yard bottleneck. It does not change the customs sequence, but it shortens the window in which a problem is discovered. *(Reported fact)*

**2026 — the Hormuz disruption changed the value of inland buffers.** With Dammam exposed to the strait and Jeddah absorbing diverted cargo, importers who could hold stock at Riyadh had an advantage over those who depended on a single coastal release. The dry port's 30-day window became a working capital tool, not just a storage discount. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Why it matters: an inland port in Riyadh

Riyadh is the largest consumption market in Saudi Arabia, with a metropolitan population of about 7 million and a regional population above 8.5 million. It is also the base for a growing share of the Kingdom's distribution, e-commerce and government procurement. Almost everything the city consumes arrives by truck or rail from a coastal port, and the last 400 km is where cost and uncertainty accumulate.

A dry port moves the customs and storage functions inland. Instead of clearing at Dammam, paying storage after free time and then trucking a released container to Riyadh, the importer can move the box under bond to Riyadh, clear it there and deliver it from a yard inside the city. That has three advantages: the inland leg is priced earlier, the storage buffer is longer, and the final delivery is shorter.

It also has three costs. The rail slot must exist. The bond must be in place. And the container must be rail-compatible, which rules out some overweight, out-of-gauge and dangerous cargo. The dry port is not a universal substitute for coastal clearance; it is a specialised tool for full containers moving to a Riyadh distribution point.

The economics are easiest to see in a comparison. A container cleared at Dammam and trucked to Riyadh pays terminal handling, a truck leg and a delivery window. A container railed to Riyadh pays terminal handling, rail haulage, dry-port handling and a short truck leg. Rail is usually cheaper per FEU for a full container going to one warehouse; truck is faster and more flexible for multi-drop delivery.

The risk profile also differs. A coastal port is exposed to vessel bunching, yard congestion and equipment shortages. An inland port is exposed to rail pathing, bond validity and inspection capacity. Neither is risk-free; they fail in different ways.

## How the Dammam–Riyadh corridor works

The corridor has five steps.

**1. Discharge at Dammam.** The container is unloaded at Terminal 1 or Terminal 2 and moved to the on-dock rail interface or the truck gate. The terminal releases the box only after the carrier's destination charges are settled and the customs status allows movement.

**2. Choose rail or road.** Rail is operated by SAR with SGP Intermodal as the commercial interface. Up to seven services a day run between Dammam and Riyadh Dry Port, with a journey time of about 4–6 hours. Truck is about 400 km and 4–5 hours on the highway, subject to driver hours, axle limits and checkpoint delays.

**3. Move under bond.** The container travels under a Saudi inland transit procedure. The bond guarantees duty and VAT if the cargo does not reach the dry port or is not cleared. The bill of lading and the customs declaration must agree on the importer, the notify party and the destination.

**4. Clear at Riyadh.** The dry port has a customs presence, inspection capacity and a bonded yard. The importer lodges the Fasah declaration, presents the SABER certificates, pays duty and VAT, and obtains the release. Inspection can be documentary, scanner-based or physical.

**5. Deliver or store.** A released container can be delivered to a Riyadh warehouse, stripped for LCL distribution or held in the bonded yard. The 30-day free storage applies to the dry port; storage after day 30 is charged per container per day.

The weak link is usually step 3. A bond that does not cover the cargo, a bill of lading that names the wrong notify party, or a carrier that will not release the box to rail can stop the corridor before the train leaves. The strong link is step 2: the rail schedule is frequent enough that a missed slot costs hours or a day, not a week, provided you rebook immediately.

## Inside Riyadh Dry Port

The site covers 918,639 m² and includes six warehouses of about 6,480 m² each. That is enough covered space for LCL, project cargo and high-value goods. The dry port also has a customs inspection area, X-ray capacity and a separate empty-container yard.

The ecosystem is broader than the dry port itself. SGP Intermodal operates Riyadh Dry Port, Riyadh Empty Yard and Dammam Empty Container Yard as one product. That matters because empty-container repositioning is a real cost in the corridor: a shipper who needs a box in Riyadh may be drawing on an empty yard in Dammam, and the repositioning charge is part of the door-to-door cost. A single operator can move empties more efficiently than three parties with three systems. It can also price the move however it likes.

The tariff is published in three sections: charges for shipping lines, charges for brokers and consignees, and rail freight. The line items include inbound handling, gate passes, empty handling, X-ray, reefer plugging, sampling and overweight handling. A bundled quote can hide a charge that a competing quote itemises. Ask for the tariff reference and the effective date.

The dry port is not a free zone. Cargo is under customs control until it is cleared. The 30-day free storage is a storage concession, not a suspension of duty, VAT or inspection. A container can sit in the bonded yard for a month and still be illegal to deliver if the SABER certificate is missing.

## The 30-day window: buffer or trap?

The April 2026 extension from 7 to 30 days is the most important change at the dry port in years. It gives importers a month to assemble documents, resolve a valuation query or wait for a product certificate. For a regulated product with a slow SABER process, that can be the difference between a manageable delay and a storage invoice.

But the window creates a false sense of safety. Three clocks run at once:

- **The dry-port storage clock.** Thirty days free, then a daily charge.
- **The customs clock.** Duty and VAT are due when the declaration is assessed. A delay in assessment does not stop the liability.
- **The carrier clock.** Demurrage and detention at the sea terminal and on the container run on the carrier's contract, not the dry port's tariff.

A container can be inside its 30-day free window at Riyadh and still be accruing demurrage at Dammam because the carrier has not received the empty back or the destination charges are unpaid. The dry port's free storage does not pause the carrier's clock. That is the trap.

The second trap is rail compatibility. The dry port's overweight limits are 25 t for a 20 ft and 30.5 t for a 40 ft or longer. A container that is legal on Saudi roads may still exceed the rail limit, especially for dense cargo. Check the gross weight before booking the rail slot.

The third trap is dangerous goods. The tariff applies a 20% surcharge to hazardous cargo, and the dry port requires carrier approval and correct documentation. DG is accepted, but it is a poor fit for a tight clearance window.

## Clearance at Riyadh Dry Port

The sequence is the same as at a coastal port, with one difference: the inspection and release happen inland.

1. **Pre-arrival declaration.** The importer or broker lodges the Fasah declaration before the container leaves Dammam. The invoice, packing list, bill of lading and certificate of origin must agree.

2. **SABER.** A product certificate must exist before a shipment certificate can issue. For regulated products, the PC is the long pole. The dry port cannot create one.

3. **Bond and transit document.** The inland bond must cover the cargo and the route. The carrier must release the container to the rail operator, not just to the consignee.

4. **Duty and VAT.** Duty is 5% on CIF; VAT is 15% on the duty-paid value. A valuation query stops the release. The importer of record carries the liability.

5. **Inspection.** Documentary, scanner or physical. Food, cosmetics, chemicals and some electronics attract lab testing. The dry port has X-ray and sampling capacity, but it is not a laboratory.

6. **Release and delivery.** Once the declaration is assessed and paid, the dry port issues the release. The container can be delivered, stripped for LCL or held in the bonded yard.

The dry port's advantage is that the importer can deal with one customs office and one yard instead of a coastal terminal and a separate inland warehouse. The disadvantage is that a problem discovered at Riyadh is 400 km from the port, and fixing it may require a document reissue that only the origin forwarder can perform.

## How to act: six decisions before you book

**1. Confirm the cargo is rail-compatible.** Check the gross weight against the 25 t / 30.5 t limits, the DG status and the dimensions. If the container is overweight or out-of-gauge, use a truck and skip the dry port.

**2. Book the rail slot with the ocean booking.** The corridor has up to seven daily services, but a peak week can fill. A slot booked after discharge is a slot you may not get. Ask for the train number and the cut-off.

**3. Put the bond in place before the vessel sails.** The bond is the legal basis for the inland move. A bond arranged after arrival is a delay, not a formality. Confirm the bond covers the HS chapter and the value.

**4. Start SABER before the container leaves China.** The PC is the long pole. A shipment certificate issued at Riyadh is still a week late if the PC does not exist. For regulated categories, use a Saudi importer of record who already holds the certificate.

**5. Decide who clears.** The importer of record can clear at Riyadh, or a broker can clear on its behalf. The dry port tariff has separate sections for shipping lines and for brokers/consignees. Make sure the quote names the payer for each line.

**6. Price the last mile.** The dry port is inside Riyadh, but the final delivery still needs a truck, a delivery window and a receiving appointment. For multiple delivery points, the truck may be cheaper than rail plus several short legs. Run the comparison before you commit.

## Cost and transit drivers

The dry port is a cost-shifting tool, not a cost-elimination tool. It moves the inland leg earlier and changes the storage economics; it does not remove the underlying charges.

**Rail haulage.** Priced per container by SAR and collected by SGP Intermodal. The driver is distance, equipment type and volume commitment. Rail is usually 10–20% cheaper per FEU than truck for a full container on the Dammam–Riyadh lane *(editorial range)*, but the gap narrows when the truck can backhaul or when the rail slot is scarce.

**Dry-port handling.** Inbound handling, gate passes, lift-on/lift-off, X-ray, reefer plugging and stripping are separate line items. For a standard FCL dry container, handling is a small share of the door-to-door cost. For LCL, stripping and cross-stuffing can dominate.

**Storage.** Thirty days free, then a daily charge per container. The risk is not the daily rate; it is the number of days. A container that misses the window by two weeks can erase the saving from the rail move.

**Inland transit.** Rail is 4–6 hours; truck is 4–5 hours. The rail move needs a day for terminal handling at both ends, so the door-to-door difference between rail and truck is often less than the schedule suggests. Rail wins on cost and predictability; truck wins on speed and flexibility.

**Clearance.** Brokerage, SABER, inspection and lab testing. A clean file clears in 2–5 days at Riyadh; a file with a certificate gap can take two weeks. The dry port's 30-day window absorbs the storage, not the compliance cost.

**Total transit.** China–Dammam is 18–26 days direct. Add 1–2 days for discharge and rail, 2–5 days for clearance and 1 day for delivery. A realistic China–Riyadh door-to-door range is 22–34 days, with the wide end driven by SABER and rail slots rather than sailing.

## Risk and compliance

**Bond risk.** An inland bond that does not cover the cargo, the route or the value can stop the move. The bond is a customs instrument, not an insurance policy. Confirm the scope in writing.

**Rail slot risk.** Seven daily services is a schedule, not a guarantee. In a peak week, the slot may be unavailable, and the fallback is a truck at a higher cost. Book early and keep a truck quote as a benchmark.

**Overweight risk.** The dry port enforces 25 t for a 20 ft and 30.5 t for a 40 ft. An overweight container can be rejected at the gate or require a permit. Check the VGM and the rail limit before booking.

**DG risk.** Hazardous cargo needs carrier approval, the correct UN number, packaging and documentation, and a 20% surcharge. The dry port is not a dedicated DG facility; expect a slower inspection and a narrower acceptance window.

**Customs risk.** Duty is assessed on CIF and VAT at 15% on the duty-paid value. A valuation query or a classification dispute can stop the release. The importer of record carries the liability, and an aggressive DDP structure can create a retroactive assessment.

**Concentration risk.** SGP runs the Dammam terminals, the rail interface and the Riyadh ecosystem. That is efficient, but it means a single operator's system outage or labour dispute can affect both ends of the corridor. Keep a truck fallback and a second broker.

**Storage-clock risk.** The 30-day free window is a concession, not a right. The tariff can change, and the free period applies to specific cargo categories. Confirm the current terms at booking, not at arrival.

## Our read

**Riyadh Dry Port is the best inland clearance tool in Saudi Arabia, and it is oversold as a storage solution.** The rail link, the customs presence and the 30-day window make it genuinely useful for Riyadh-bound FCL. They do not make it a free warehouse, and they do not fix a missing SABER certificate.

**Use it when you have volume, a single destination and a clean file.** A full container moving to one Riyadh warehouse is the ideal case. A multi-drop delivery, an overweight box or a DG shipment is a poor fit. The dry port rewards planning and punishes improvisation.

**The 30-day window is a working-capital tool.** For an importer with a slow customs process, a month of free storage can be worth more than a small reduction in freight. For an importer with a clean file, the window is irrelevant; the container clears in days and the rail move is the real saving.

**Watch the operator concentration.** SGP now controls the sea terminal, the rail interface and the inland node. That is good for coordination and bad for contingency. A serious supply-chain plan names a second route, even if it is only a truck and a coastal clearance.

**The corridor will get better before it gets cheaper.** The expansion, the five corridors and the TOS investment will add capacity and visibility. They will not remove the bond, the rail slot or the customs sequence. Those are the constraints to manage. *(Editorial judgment)*

## FAQ

**What is Riyadh Dry Port?**
An inland container terminal and customs facility in Al-Malaz/As-Sinaiyah, Riyadh, built in 1982. It covers 918,639 m², has six warehouses and is the largest dry port in Saudi Arabia.

**How long is free storage at Riyadh Dry Port?**
Thirty days for various goods and transit containers, extended from seven days on 23 April 2026. The free window applies at the dry port; carrier demurrage and terminal storage at Dammam are separate.

**Can I clear customs at Riyadh instead of Dammam?**
Yes, if the container moves under an inland bond and the carrier releases it to the rail operator. The importer of record remains liable for duty and VAT.

**Is rail cheaper than truck from Dammam to Riyadh?**
Usually, for a full container moving to a single destination. The rail rate is often 10–20% below truck per FEU *(editorial range)*, but the gap depends on volume, backhaul and slot availability. For multiple delivery points, truck can be cheaper overall.

**What are the overweight limits at Riyadh Dry Port?**
25 t for a 20 ft and 30.5 t for a 40 ft or longer, enforced at the dry port. Check the VGM before booking.

**Does Riyadh Dry Port accept dangerous goods?**
Yes, with carrier approval, the correct documentation and a 20% surcharge. The dry port is not a dedicated DG facility, so expect a slower inspection and a narrower acceptance window.

**How long does clearance take at Riyadh Dry Port?**
Two to five days with a complete file, up to two weeks with a SABER gap or valuation query. The 30-day window covers the delay, not the compliance cost.
