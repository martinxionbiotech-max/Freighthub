## Direct answer

Sohar is the only deepwater container gateway in the GCC that a ship can reach without entering the Strait of Hormuz. It sits on the Gulf of Oman at Liwa, about 200 km northwest of Muscat and roughly 170 km from Dubai, with an 18.5 m approach channel, 18 m harbour basin and seven container berths. In the first half of 2026 it handled 545,295 TEU, up 40% year on year, and 52 million tonnes of total cargo on the port's headline measure. ([Oman hub](/countries/oman/) has the national picture; this page is about using the terminal.)

The number to keep in mind is not 52 million. It is 545,295 TEU, because the port's own cargo table shows container volume rising while dry bulk fell. Sohar's 2026 boom is a container and breakbulk story created by the Hormuz closure, not a surge in Omani consumption. Use Sohar as a northern gateway for Oman and the UAE border, as a project-cargo port, and as your non-Hormuz contingency. Do not mistake its throughput for a market of 50 million consumers. *(Editorial judgment)*

## What changed in the last 60 days

**21 August 2026 — the H1 results landed.** Sohar reported 52 million tonnes of total cargo, up 52%, with container throughput of 545,295 TEU, up 40%, and breakbulk of 1.28 million freight tonnes, more than double. The port attributed the container gain primarily to transshipment. That is the single most important qualifier in the announcement. *(Reported fact)*

**29 June 2026 — CMA CGM and Asyad signed a US$400 million framework agreement.** The project is a multipurpose logistics terminal at Sohar, sized for capacity outside the Strait of Hormuz. It is the first major private commitment built for the post-crisis routing pattern rather than the pre-crisis one. It is a framework agreement, not a completed terminal. *(Reported fact)*

**October 2026 — a new chief executive.** Dr Raid Al Rubaiey succeeded Emile Hoogsteden as CEO of SOHAR Port and Freezone. A management change at a joint-venture port usually precedes a strategy refresh, a tariff review or both. Watch the next annual report for the direction. *(Reported fact)*

**12 October 2026 — a tanker was attacked near Sohar.** A drone strike hit an oil tanker in the waters near the port; two sailors were reported missing. Sohar is outside Hormuz, but it is not outside the conflict. War-risk insurance for a Sohar call is cheaper than for a Gulf transit, not free. *(Reported fact)*

**4 November 2026 — the freezone won an award.** SOHAR Freezone was named the best industrial zone in the Middle East and fourth globally for 2026. Awards do not move cargo, but they signal that the freezone is winning tenants against Jebel Ali, Khalifa and Duqm. *(Reported fact)*

**8 November 2026 — six investment agreements worth OMR 63 million.** The freezone signed the deals at an OPAZ ceremony. The sectors and tenants matter less than the pattern: the freezone is converting industrial land into leases while the container terminal converts diverted boxes into volume. Both are real; only one is cyclical. *(Reported fact)*

**April–September 2026 — Hafeet Rail advanced.** The US$2.5 billion Oman–UAE railway reached about 40% completion in April, and by September the target for trial runs had been set for late 2027. The rail link will connect Sohar to Abu Dhabi and the UAE network. It is not open, and it will not carry your 2026 cargo. *(Reported fact)*

**August 2026 — Marsa LNG slipped.** The US$1.6 billion LNG bunkering project at Sohar moved its start to mid-2028. The project is still building; the date is later. For shippers, this is a signal about the port's capital programme, not about current capacity. *(Reported fact)*

## Why it matters: the only GCC box gate outside Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz is a single point of failure for five of the six GCC states. Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Saudi Arabia's eastern ports and the UAE's Gulf coast all depend on it. Oman does not. Sohar sits on the Gulf of Oman, outside the strait, with deep water and a direct road to the UAE.

The geography became a commercial fact in 2026. After the strait shut at the end of February, Bahrain lost about four fifths of its monthly import containers. Over the same half-year, Sohar's container throughput climbed 40%. Two GCC states, one crisis, opposite outcomes — the market ran the routing experiment for us.

The benefit is concentrated in the north. Sohar is within trucking range of Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Al Ain and the Saudi Eastern Province. Salalah, 1,000 km south on the Arabian Sea, is a relay hub with almost no gateway market. Duqm is a project-cargo bet. A quote that names "Oman" without a port is not a quote.

But geography explains the opportunity, not the limits. Sohar's container capacity is about 2.46 million TEU. Its 2025 volume was 845,622 TEU. Even after a 40% surge, 2026 is running at roughly 1.1 million TEU annualised — less than half of capacity. The port has headroom. What it does not have is density: fewer direct China calls than Jebel Ali, a thinner feeder market, and a road border that turns every UAE delivery into a second import.

## The capacity picture: 2.46 million TEU, seven berths, 18 metres

Sohar was built as an industrial port, and the container terminal is a later layer. That history shows in the layout.

**The channel and basin are deep.** The approach channel is 18.5 m, the harbour basin 18 m. Berths 10 and 11 take vessels to 17.7 m draft; berths 12, 13 and 14 take 17.0 m and 380 m length overall. That is deep enough for the largest container ships calling the Gulf, and it is why Sohar can absorb diverted mainline capacity without dredging.

**The container terminal is concentrated.** Oman International Container Terminal operates the box berths, with a capacity reported at 1.5 million TEU after its last expansion. The port puts total container capacity at about 2.46 million TEU across seven berths and 2.4 km of quay, which is a real gateway rather than a niche.

**Utilisation is low.** At 845,622 TEU in 2025 against 2.46 million TEU of capacity, the terminal ran at about a third of its design. Even the 2026 surge leaves it below half. The constraint is not quay length. It is the number of services, the feeder network and the landside connections.

**The mix is industrial.** The port's 2025 cargo table shows 61.1 million freight tonnes of dry, break and liquid bulk against 8.2 million freight tonnes of containers. Containers are about 12% of freight tonnes, even though they are the fastest-growing segment. Sohar is a bulk and liquid port with a container terminal attached, not the other way round.

**The freezone is the land bank.** About 45 km² of freezone sits behind the port, with 4,500 ha in the wider port-and-freezone envelope. That land is why Sohar can host refineries, metals, food processing and logistics parks that a pure container terminal could not. It is also why the port's statistics include industrial flows that a container-focused reader should strip out.

## The CMA CGM–Asyad terminal: what it is, when it matters

The US$400 million terminal is the most important new project at Sohar since the port opened. It is also the most commonly misread.

**What was signed.** A framework agreement between Asyad Group and CMA CGM, announced on 29 June 2026, for a multipurpose logistics terminal at Sohar. Trade press framed it as capacity outside the Strait of Hormuz. The project is a terminal, not a shipping service: it does not by itself add a China call.

**What it changes.** A multipurpose terminal can handle containers, breakbulk and project cargo in one facility. That fits Sohar's actual cargo mix better than a pure box terminal. If CMA CGM anchors the facility with its own services, Sohar gains a carrier commitment that a landlord port cannot buy. That is the strategic prize.

**What it does not change.** The timeline. A framework agreement is not a construction contract, a dredging permit or a berth handover. As of November 2026 there is no public operational date. Do not build a 2027 routing on the assumption that the terminal is working.

**What to watch.** Three milestones matter: the final investment decision, the first pile in the water, and the first vessel worked. The first is an announcement, the second is a construction risk, and the third is the only one that changes your schedule. Until the third, Sohar's existing seven berths are the product.

## Hafeet Rail and the land bridge that is not open yet

The Oman–UAE railway is the other project that could change Sohar's economics. It is a US$2.5 billion link, about 40% complete in April 2026, with trial runs targeted for late 2027. When it opens, it will connect Sohar to Abu Dhabi and the UAE's rail network.

The commercial logic is straightforward. A container discharged at Sohar could move by rail to an inland terminal in the UAE instead of by truck. Rail is cheaper per tonne-km at volume, less exposed to border queues and more predictable in bad weather. For a Chinese exporter shipping to a UAE distribution centre, that could make Sohar a genuine alternative to Jebel Ali for the first time.

The caveats are just as important. Rail does not create a customs union. A container that leaves Sohar by rail for Abu Dhabi is still an import into the UAE, with a UAE declaration, UAE duty and UAE conformity. The border moves from a road checkpoint to a rail terminal; it does not disappear. The rail also has to be commercially operated, with a schedule that matches vessel arrivals. A train that runs twice a week is not a land bridge.

Until the trial runs become a published timetable, treat Hafeet Rail as a 2028 story. It belongs in your scenario planning, not in your 2027 freight budget. *(Editorial judgment)*

## How to act: six decisions before you book

**1. Name the Omani market you are actually serving.** Sohar for the north, Muscat and the UAE border. Salalah only when the consignee is in Dhofar or the forwarder can show the onward leg and its cost. Duqm for project cargo. They are different products, and a quote that says "Oman" without a port has not quoted anything.

**2. Get the direct call in writing.** China–Sohar is commonly 18–24 days on a direct rotation. If the carrier's rotation is a relay through Jebel Ali, Colombo or Singapore, the connection time is the swing factor, not the ocean leg. Name the hub, the feeder and the cut-off in the booking. A "Sohar rate" that is really a Jebel Ali rate plus a feeder is a different product.

**3. Price the road leg and the border before the ocean leg.** Sohar to Dubai is about 170 km; Sohar to Muscat about 200 km; Sohar to Riyadh roughly 1,200 km. The first is a day truck, the second is a long day, the third is a corridor. A container cleared in Oman and delivered to the UAE is an import into the UAE, with its own declaration, duty and conformity. The GCC customs union has never functioned as a true single market for goods.

**4. Sort out who files, not just who pays.** Bayan is a company system: the Omani importer needs a commercial registration, a PKI token and a named user, and the declaration is lodged by a licensed broker. A Chinese seller can quote DDP, but it cannot be the importer of record without an Omani entity. Write the importer and the broker into the contract before the first booking.

**5. Open the conformity file at the sample stage.** Regulated products need a CoC or a type-examination certificate under the Omani Conformity Scheme, and the document runs for twelve months. Electricals and appliances sit at the centre of the list, which keeps expanding in small increments. Waiting until the vessel sails turns a certificate into warehousing cost.

**6. Budget the VAT timing, not just the rate.** A registered importer pays 5% at the border and recovers it later, so the cost is financing rather than duty. On a 40-foot box of mid-value goods the cash sits for months. That is why landed-price deals are common in Muscat: the importer is selling you its balance sheet as much as its licence.

## Cost and transit drivers

**The ocean rate is a range, and the relay is the variable.** China–Sohar direct calls are fewer than China–Jebel Ali calls, so the direct rate is usually higher per box. A relay through Jebel Ali can be cheaper on the ocean leg and more expensive on the connection, the hub handling and the second documentation set. Compare door to door, with the relay broken out.

**Sohar avoids the Hormuz premium, not all risk.** By mid-2026, hull war-risk cover for a strait transit was being quoted at 3.5–7.5% of value, based on industry estimates that Singapore's transport ministry cited, while carriers added US$1,500–4,000 per TEU in Gulf surcharges. A Sohar call sidesteps the strait leg of that bill. It does not sidestep the October 2026 tanker attack near the port, the general loading on Oman, or the cost of a network that has been rerouted around a war.

**The port's cargo mix drives the tariff.** Sohar handles very large ore carriers, Valemax-class bulkers and liquid bulk at dedicated jetties. Those berths have their own tariffs, drafts and equipment. A container quote that uses a bulk berth's productivity assumption will be wrong. Ask for the terminal, the berth and the handling rate separately.

**The truck is cheap; the second border is not.** Sohar to Dubai is a day's drive, and the kilometre rate is unremarkable. The cost jumps at the UAE entry: a fresh declaration, 5% duty, 5% VAT, a conformity file and a UAE importer of record. Price the border as a fixed cost per shipment, not as a line in the road rate.

**Air freight into Muscat is a niche, and a thin one.** Belly capacity exists on the scheduled carriers; dedicated freighter space is scarce. Most urgent China–Oman cargo actually lands in Dubai and moves by truck, which is quicker than waiting for a Muscat freighter and adds a customs entry. Treat air as an exception for launch stock and spares, not as a seasonal plan.

**Transit ranges.** China–Sohar is commonly 18–24 days direct and 24–32 days relayed, port to port, before clearance and trucking. Add two to five days for a CoC inspection if the product is regulated, and add the road leg for UAE or Saudi delivery. The ranges are industry claims, not a sailing schedule.

## Clearance at Sohar: Bayan, duty, VAT and the CoC

Oman's import regime is simple on tax and demanding on documentation.

**The tax stack is short.** Customs duty is 5% of CIF for the general tariff. VAT is 5%, but it is levied on the duty-paid value, which lifts the effective charge to roughly 5.25% of the invoice. Excise sits in front of both for tobacco, alcohol, energy drinks, carbonated drinks and pork, at 50–100% of the import value.

**Bayan.** The single window is company-based. The importer needs a commercial registration, a PKI token and an authorised user; the declaration runs on 12-digit HS codes. Individuals cannot self-file. A Chinese seller quoting DDP without an Omani importer of record is quoting a service it cannot legally perform.

**Conformity is a product passport.** Regulated goods enter on a CoC or a type-examination certificate issued under the Omani scheme, and it expires after a year. Low-voltage electricals and appliances are the centre of gravity; water dispensers and e-scooters joined the list in 2026. Because the certificate follows the product rather than the shipment, the renewal date is part of the launch plan.

**The freezone is a separate customs space.** Goods entering SOHAR Freezone are not duty-paid in the same way as goods entering Oman. That is the point of the freezone. But when goods leave the freezone into Oman, duty and VAT apply; when they are re-exported, the documentation has to prove it. Keep freezone and domestic inventories in separate accounts, because a commingled stock is a customs dispute.

**The military and security layer.** Sohar is a civilian port with a large industrial base, including refineries and strategic metals. Security inspections are real, and the October 2026 tanker attack shows that the seaward approach is not immune. Build a security buffer into the schedule for project cargo and dangerous goods.

## Risk and compliance: where Sohar shipments break

**A port statistic read as a market size.** The 545,295 TEU headline describes boxes handled, not boxes consumed in Oman. Most of the increase was relay cargo. A brand that builds inventory against the port number will overstock a country of roughly five million people. Fix: ask for the gateway-versus-transshipment split before you set a sales forecast.

**The "one Gulf market" shortcut.** Clearing in Sohar does not make a container free to enter Dubai. The UAE runs its own entry, its own duty and its own product checks. Fix: plan every cross-border move as a new import, with a named UAE importer and a separate declaration.

**The certificate that missed the boat.** The factory ships a regulated appliance on the assumption that the importer will "sort out the CoC". The container lands, the certificate does not, and a rushed type examination replaces a planned one. Fix: make the certificate a booking prerequisite and put its expiry date in the launch calendar.

**The DDP contract with no Omani filer.** A Chinese seller signs a delivered-price deal, then finds that Bayan needs a registered importer with PKI access and a licensed broker. Borrowing a local agent's licence may move the liability and the duty with it. Fix: name the importer of record and the broker before the contract is signed, not after the first hold.

**The freezone exit with a thin paper trail.** Cargo leaves the freezone for a third country, but the file cannot prove re-export. The fallback is duty on the full value. Fix: keep the entry declaration, the re-export declaration and the transport document together, and reconcile them before the container leaves.

**The railway that is still a target date.** A 2027 budget assumes Hafeet Rail is carrying boxes. The public target is trial runs in late 2027; a trial is not a published service. Fix: cost the road move as the base case and treat rail as a future saving, not a current rate.

**The terminal that has not been built.** A 2027 plan assumes the CMA CGM facility is working. The public record stops at a framework agreement and a headline value; there is no operational date. Fix: book against the seven existing container berths and treat the new terminal as upside capacity.

## Our read: a structural hedge with a cyclical headline

**Do** give Sohar a permanent place in your routing options, not a crisis-only bookmark. For a Gulf programme large enough to support two gateways, it is the most credible box port outside Hormuz, with deep water, a working freezone and demonstrated ability to absorb diverted volume. The strategic value is the option itself, even in a quiet year.

**Do** use Sohar for what it is good at: northern Oman, the UAE border, project cargo, industrial inputs and contingency. The port's industrial base — refinery, metals, food, chemicals — is the reason it exists, and it gives a forwarder more cargo options than a pure box terminal.

**Do** put the compliance work on the critical path early. Duty and VAT are round numbers; the CoC file and the Bayan registration are not. Launches rarely fail on the tax rate. They fail on a document that nobody owned.

**Avoid** turning the 52 million tonne headline into a container forecast. The port's detailed table shows 27.4 million freight tonnes of port cargo in the first half, with dry bulk lower and containers higher. The two figures use different measures. Container growth is genuine; the bulk decline is equally genuine.

**Avoid** equating Omani port growth with Omani spending. The container surge was mostly relay cargo. For a consumer brand, the addressable population is about five million, not the 2.46 million TEU of terminal capacity behind the fence.

**Avoid** planning a 2028 supply chain around Hafeet Rail or the CMA CGM terminal until the first is a published timetable and the second is a working berth. Both are credible projects with real money behind them. Neither is a 2026 or 2027 product.

## FAQ

**Is Sohar Port outside the Strait of Hormuz?**
Yes. Sohar is on the Gulf of Oman, outside the strait. A vessel calling Sohar does not transit Hormuz, which is why the port became the GCC's main contingency in 2026.

**How long does sea freight from China to Sohar take?**
Commonly 18–24 days direct, port to port, and 24–32 days relayed. Add clearance and trucking. The ranges are industry claims, not a sailing schedule.

**Is Sohar a transshipment port?**
It is both, but the 2026 container growth was primarily transshipment. The port handles Omani gateway cargo and relay cargo; the relay share is what surged when Hormuz closed.

**What is the draft at Sohar?**
The approach channel is 18.5 m and the harbour basin 18 m. Berths 10 and 11 take 17.7 m; berths 12–14 take 17.0 m and 380 m length overall. Confirm the berth-specific limit for your vessel.

**What are the import duties and taxes in Oman?**
Five percent duty on CIF for most goods, five percent VAT on the duty-paid value, and excise on tobacco, alcohol, energy drinks, carbonated drinks and pork. The combined effective rate on CIF is about 5.25%.

**Do I need a certificate of conformity for Oman?**
Yes, for regulated products. The Omani Conformity Scheme requires a CoC or a type examination certificate, valid for one year. Low-voltage electricals and appliances are the core scope, and the list is expanding.

**Can I use Sohar as a gateway to Dubai or Saudi Arabia?**
Yes, by road. Sohar to Dubai is about 170 km. But the UAE or Saudi import is a separate customs regime with its own duty, VAT and conformity. The GCC common tariff is not a customs union in practice.

**What is the CMA CGM–Asyad terminal?**
A US$400 million multipurpose logistics terminal at Sohar, announced as a framework agreement in June 2026. It is not yet built and has no public operational date. It is a capacity project, not a current service.

**What is Hafeet Rail?**
A US$2.5 billion Oman–UAE railway link, about 40% complete in April 2026, with trial runs targeted for late 2027. It will connect Sohar to Abu Dhabi. It is not open for freight yet.
