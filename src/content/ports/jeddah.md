## Direct answer

Jeddah Islamic Port is Saudi Arabia's largest container gateway and, in 2026, the peninsula's pressure valve: Hormuz disruption pushed Gulf-bound cargo onto its berths, so the yard and truck gate became the constraint. Expect 18–26 days from China, then 5–12 days of clearance. Fix terminal, free time and the 15-day rule before booking.

## What changed in the last 60 days

- **14 September 2026** — Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd shifted four more Gemini services (AE5, AE11, AE12, ME2) to Suez. AE15, which already calls Jeddah, was the template. More Suez transits mean more direct Jeddah calls.
- **18 September 2026** — Port of NEOM said its Terminal 1 container facility is still expected to open this year, a potential northern Red Sea alternative for project cargo.
- **14 October 2026** — Suez Canal container transits passed 50% of pre-disruption levels, confirming the Red Sea return is structural.
- **22 October 2026** — Mawani opened an investment opportunity for the Al-Khumrah integrated logistics zone, signalling that the port's next phase is distribution, not more quay.
- **24 October 2026** — Emirates Shipping Line announced the China Red Sea Connector for November: four ships of about 2,500 TEU on a roughly fortnightly China–Red Sea rotation.

Congestion eased from its June–August peak but has not disappeared: The Loadstar reported on 19 August 2026 that CMA CGM relaxed a cargo restriction while congestion persisted.

## Why Jeddah's dual role matters

Jeddah does two jobs that look compatible on a map and compete in reality.

The first is a **consumption gateway**: about two-thirds of Saudi seaborne imports pass through Jeddah, serving more than 30 million people and the holy cities. This cargo wants fast release.

The second is a **pressure valve**: when Hormuz is disrupted, cargo for the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Iraq can discharge at Jeddah and move overland. Hapag-Lloyd issued detailed requirements for this flow in August 2026.

Both jobs use the same yard, truck gate and inspection capacity. When the valve opens, gateway cargo queues behind transit cargo and average dwell becomes meaningless. Dwell depends on which flow you are in and which terminal you land at — so the useful question is not "how congested is Jeddah?" but "which terminal, which flow, which week?"

## Terminals: why SCT and RSGT behave differently

**South Container Terminal (SCT)**, operated by DP World, handled more than 221,200 TEU in July 2026 — its strongest month since 1999 — with exports at 79,720 TEU. First-half throughput rose 79% year on year across imports, exports and transshipment, with 390 vessel calls. DP World has added three STS cranes to reach 17, plus 17 automated electric RTGs, 35 electric terminal tractors and 30 trailers, and is targeting 5 million TEU of annual capacity. APM Terminals agreed in February 2026 to acquire a stake in SCT, tying it closer to Maersk's network.

**The RSGT complex** is the other main operator and the platform for the next capacity phase. On 26 August 2026, RSGT and CMA CGM signed definitive agreements in Paris for Terminal 4: a US$434 million initial investment adding up to 2.6 million TEU, with deep-water berths and 10 STS cranes.

Three consequences. Berth windows differ by terminal, so a carrier's proforma assumes a specific one. Free time and storage tariffs are terminal-specific, which makes "Jeddah" without a terminal name an incomparable quote. And equipment investment is terminal-specific: DP World's July record partly reflects its crane and RTG additions.

## Why the landside is the real port

Most containers leave by truck for western Saudi, or by rail and truck to [Riyadh Dry Port](/ports/riyadh-dry-port/) for the centre. The gate is constrained by appointments, inspection capacity and the geometry of a port inside a city. In July 2026 the government opened a truck marshalling area at the port, billed as the world's largest, and The Loadstar reported on 3 July 2026 that congestion was hobbling the land bridge — the mechanism that makes Jeddah strategically useful.

**Free time is shorter than you think.** Free days are consumed by clearance, not sailing. A missing SABER certificate turns them into a countdown to a storage invoice.

**Relocation is now explicit.** Hapag-Lloyd's August 2026 rules warn that containers may be moved if cargo remains uncleared, at the shipper's cost — the port will clear its yard at your expense.

**The 15-day clock is real.** Transit cargo discharged from 1 August 2026 must be cleared and moved onward within 15 days, confirmed in writing before booking. Miss it and you are in a negotiation you will not win.

## Clearance at Jeddah: the sequence that decides your week

1. **Pre-arrival file.** Invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, insurance and SABER documents go through Fasah before arrival. Invoice and packing list must agree line by line.
2. **SABER validation.** The product certificate must exist before a shipment certificate can issue. For regulated products this is the long pole, and a PC cannot be created at the port.
3. **Duty and VAT assessment.** Duty on CIF value at the tariff line; VAT at 15% on the duty-inclusive value. A valuation query stops the clock.
4. **Inspection.** Green channel releases on documents; yellow and red require physical or scanner inspection. [Food](/sectors/food/), cosmetics, chemicals and [electronics](/sectors/electronics/) attract lab testing.
5. **Payment and delivery order.** Destination charges are settled, increasingly through ODeX Saudi Arabia. No payment, no gate pass.
6. **Truck appointment and gate-out.** A released container without a truck slot is still a container in the yard.

The [customs clearance guide](/guides/customs-clearance/) covers the document set; the [certification guide](/guides/certification/) covers SABER scope.

## How to act: four steps before you book

**1. Name the terminal and the rotation.** Ask which terminal the service calls, which free days apply and whether the box can be rolled. A quote without a terminal is a range, not an offer.

**2. Fix the importer of record and the payer.** For transit cargo, the notify party and payer must be Saudi-based, and the bill of lading must carry the transit clause. Confirm this before the booking, not at document submission.

**3. Clear SABER before the container sails.** The product certificate is the long pole; the shipment certificate is the short one. Starting after arrival is starting a week late.

**4. Write the 15-day undertaking into the deal.** Get the letter of indemnity or written confirmation, and price relocation and storage risk into your onward rate.

## Cost and transit drivers at Jeddah

Jeddah pricing has three layers.

**Ocean and risk.** Base freight plus BAF plus any war-risk or congestion surcharge. In the March 2026 escalation, CMA CGM and Hapag-Lloyd announced emergency conflict surcharges of US$3,000/FEU for Persian Gulf and Red Sea cargo, and MSC applied US$800 per container to Persian Gulf shipments *(reported by Flexport)*. The levels moved; the structure did not. Risk is priced per box, which hurts low-value dense cargo most.

**Destination.** Terminal handling, documentation, release, storage and demurrage. Two weeks past free time can exceed the ocean freight on low-value goods.

**Inland.** Trucking to Riyadh, Jeddah, Makkah or Madinah, plus waiting time. Capacity tightens around Hajj and Umrah. A fixed inland rate without a waiting-time clause will be renegotiated at the gate.

Transit time is layered the same way: 18–26 days port to port, plus 5–12 days clearance and delivery, plus the reliability discount. A carrier advertising 20 days that rolls one sailing in four is a 27-day planning number. For mode trade-offs, see [sea versus air](/compare/sea-vs-air/) and the [FCL guide](/guides/fcl/).

## Terminal and hinterland: Riyadh, Makkah, and the land bridge

**Riyadh** is the main inland destination, served by the land bridge and [Riyadh Dry Port](/ports/riyadh-dry-port/). Inland clearance can be cheaper and faster, but only if the rail slot, inland bond and terminal release align; otherwise you have added a handover without removing a queue.

**Makkah and Madinah** make Jeddah their default door, though religious traffic compresses labour and road capacity in season.

**The Eastern Province** is served more efficiently by [Dammam](/ports/dammam/). The land bridge lets cargo cross the peninsula when the Gulf route is disrupted, but it is strategic, not cheap: the move adds cost and days and is justified only when Hormuz risk or Dammam congestion is worse.

For destination comparisons see [Jeddah vs Dammam](/compare/jeddah-vs-dammam/); for the national picture see the [Saudi Arabia country hub](/countries/saudi-arabia/).

## Risk and compliance

**Terminal ambiguity.** A quote that does not name the terminal is not comparable. Insist on terminal, free days and storage tariff in writing.

**SABER timing.** A missing product certificate cannot be fixed at the port. It converts a freight delay into an open-ended compliance delay.

**Transit documentation.** For cargo moving through Jeddah to the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain or Iraq, the bill of lading must carry the transit clause, the notify party must be Saudi-based, the payer must be Saudi-based and onward movement must be confirmed within 15 days.

**Valuation and classification.** Duty is assessed on CIF, and reclassification can raise duty and VAT retroactively. The importer of record carries the liability, which is why aggressive DDP valuation eventually damages the customer relationship. See the [DDP guide](/guides/ddp/).

**Security and war risk.** Jeddah is outside Hormuz but inside the Red Sea risk zone. Attacks around Bab al-Mandeb continued into November 2026, and war-risk surcharges can return with days of notice.

## Our read

**Jeddah is the right default for western and central Saudi, and the wrong default for the Eastern Province.** Its scale and hinterland are unbeatable for Riyadh, Makkah and Madinah; for Dammam, Jubail and Al Khobar, the land bridge is a contingency, not a strategy.

**Name the terminal before you name the rate.** SCT and RSGT differ in equipment, free time and release behaviour. The August record at SCT and the Terminal 4 investment at RSGT both signal a port investing, but they are not interchangeable.

**Price the landside, not the ocean.** In a normal week the ocean leg is predictable; in a congestion week, storage, relocation and truck waiting time decide whether the shipment makes money. Build a 10% contingency and a 7–10 day clearance tail into every quote.

**Do not treat the Red Sea recovery as finished.** Suez transits passed 50% of pre-disruption levels in October 2026, but the same month brought renewed attacks around Hormuz and continued Houthi pressure near Bab al-Mandeb. A resilient plan has a tested alternative, not a single assumption.

**Avoid Jeddah transit cargo unless you control the Saudi leg.** If you cannot name a Saudi notify party, a Saudi payer and a trucker who will move the box within 15 days, book direct to the destination port instead.

## FAQ

**How many container terminals does Jeddah have?**
Two main complexes today — DP World's South Container Terminal and the RSGT terminal — with Terminal 4 under development by RSGT and CMA CGM. General cargo, bulk, ro-ro and project cargo use separate berths.

**How long does clearance take at Jeddah?**
With a complete file, a few days. With a SABER gap or valuation query, one to three weeks. Plan 5–12 days from discharge to delivery and treat anything faster as upside.

**Is Jeddah congested in November 2026?**
Less than in July–August, when surcharges and booking curbs were in force. The landside remains the constraint and transit rules are enforced. Check the [update feed](/updates/) before a peak-season booking.

**Can I clear cargo at Riyadh instead of Jeddah?**
Yes, through Riyadh Dry Port, if the container moves under an inland transit procedure and the bond is arranged. It can be faster and cheaper, but it depends on rail slots and terminal release.

**What is the 15-day rule?**
For transit cargo discharged at Jeddah from 1 August 2026, the customer must clear and move the container onward within 15 days, confirmed in writing before booking. Carriers may relocate uncleared containers at the shipper's cost.

**Does Jeddah handle lithium batteries and dangerous goods?**
Yes, subject to carrier acceptance, UN38.3 documentation and terminal DG rules. Jeddah is stricter than many Asian ports on documentation, and DG cargo is a poor fit for a tight clearance window. See the [battery sector guide](/sectors/batteries/).
