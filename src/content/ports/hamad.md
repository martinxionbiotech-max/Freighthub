## Direct answer

Hamad Port is a capacity-rich, service-poor gateway. The terminal can berth two 1,200-metre ships at 17 m alongside and is designed for 7.5 million TEU a year, yet every Qatari port combined moved 493,105 TEU in the first half of 2026 — roughly one-seventh of that design figure. ([Qatar hub](/countries/qatar/) has the national picture; this page is about using the terminal.)

The binding constraint is not the quay. It is the 14-metre maximum draft in the approach channel, the small number of direct China rotations, and the fact that all of them transit the Strait of Hormuz. Book Hamad as a direct call only when the carrier confirms the rotation in writing. Otherwise price it as a relay through [Jebel Ali](/ports/jebel-ali/), [Salalah](/ports/salalah/) or Colombo, and put the connection days in your delivery promise rather than in your buffer. *(Editorial judgment)*

## What changed in the last 60 days

**18 March 2026 — port fees were cut, quietly.** Mwani Qatar extended free storage for transshipment cargo (containers, RoRo, general cargo) from 7–10 days to up to 30 days at Hamad and Al Ruwais, and halved RoRo transshipment handling charges, "until further notice." This is the most useful commercial fact on this page for anyone building a relay product, and it does not appear on a rate sheet. *(Reported fact)*

**4 June 2026 — free zones got customs rules.** The Cabinet approved a Finance Ministry decision giving the free zones a defined customs treatment instead of case-by-case practice, reducing the risk of a reinterpretation at the gate. *(Reported fact)*

**July–October 2026 — volumes recovered, mix shifted.** Qatar cleared 493,105 TEU in H1, then 54,086 TEU in September and more than 59,000 TEU in October; vessel calls rose from 160 to 249 month on month and general cargo jumped 64% to over 66,000 tonnes. That mix — less container, more project and bulk — fits the LNG build-out. *(Reported fact)*

**15 November 2026 — a tariff edit.** Duty on selected fresh and chilled chicken rises from 5% to 18%. Narrow, but it confirms the integrated tariff is being edited line by line. Any 2025 duty table is stale. *(Reported fact)*

## Why it matters: the 14-metre channel behind the 17-metre berth

QTerminals publishes two numbers that are easy to confuse: 17 m draft alongside at CT1 and CT2, and 14 m maximum permitted draft in the channel. The berth is deeper than the approach. That means the largest ship that can physically lie at the terminal is limited by what can float over the bar, not by what can be moored.

In practice Hamad takes the neo-Panamax and Super Post-Panamax classes that dominate Asia–Middle East loops, but not a fully laden 20,000 TEU ship at maximum draft. The cranes handle 22 rows and quad-lift, so crane productivity is not the bottleneck. Water depth is, and depth sets the schedule frequency the port can support. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

Two consequences follow. Carriers trim or lighten for the call, and recover the cost in the rate. And when a rotation is upsized, Hamad tends to be the port that gets relayed rather than the one that gets the bigger ship. Ask whether your booking is a head-haul call or a feeder connection; the answer moves transit by one to two weeks. *(Editorial judgment)*

## CT1 and CT2: two terminals, one operator, different jobs

**CT1** is the working terminal: 1,200 m of quay, 17 m alongside, 352,000 m² of stacking, 2 million TEU rated, 1,250 reefer plugs, Super Post-Panamax cranes with twin, tandem and quad lift, and Navis N4 as the terminal operating system. It also hosts the container freight station — Shed 1 for LCL stuffing and examination, Shed 3 for IMDG and FCL stripping. If you ship [LCL](/guides/lcl/), this is where your box is opened. *(Reported fact)*

**CT2** sits immediately north: 1,200 m of berth, 403,500 m², another 2 million TEU rated, built in stages with remote quay cranes and hybrid RTGs. It is the answer to a demand problem that has not arrived. On a normal week you do not choose between CT1 and CT2 — the carrier and terminal do. But a box booked to "Hamad" can discharge at either, and the haul between them is short, not free. *(Reported fact)*

The single-operator structure matters. QTerminals is 51% Mwani Qatar and 49% Milaha, so the port authority and the country's largest logistics group are the same counterparty at the gate. Decisions — a berth window, a storage waiver, a reefer plug — move faster than at a landlord port with three competing operators. There is also no competitive benchmark inside the port: you negotiate with the operator and the regulator's majority-owned vehicle at once. *(Editorial judgment)*

For cargo profiles, Hamad's reefer and project capacity suits [food](/sectors/food/), [electronics](/sectors/electronics/) and [machinery](/sectors/machinery/) rebuilds; for urgent spares, compare [air freight](/guides/air-freight/) before assuming sea is cheaper door-to-door.

## What the port fee discount actually covers

The relief is narrower than "port fees are down" and wider than "storage is free": it applies to transshipment cargo at Hamad and Al Ruwais, not to imports for the local market, and it runs "until further notice" rather than for a fixed term. Treat it as revocable, and get the tariff in writing. *(Reported fact)*

**The commercial reading.** Mwani Qatar is buying relay volume. A country with 7.5 million TEU of design capacity and about one million TEU of cargo needs transshipment to fill the terminal, and transshipment is footloose. If you route project cargo or RoRo to Iraq or the Upper Gulf, ask for the transshipment rate in writing rather than accepting a bundled relay price. *(Editorial judgment)*

## How to act: five decisions before you book

**1. Get the terminal, rotation and cargo limit in writing.** Ask for the vessel, the rotation, the terminal (CT1 or CT2), head-haul or feeder, and the maximum cargo weight the carrier will accept for the call. A heavy cargo — machinery, tiles, bagged material — can be rolled at the load port even after the rate desk accepts the booking. *(Industry claim)*

**2. Decide whether you are a Doha import or a relay.** A direct call with local clearance is one product; a relay through [Jebel Ali](/ports/jebel-ali/), Salalah or Colombo with 30 days of free transshipment storage is another. Do not mix them in one booking, and do not quote 20 days on the second. *(Editorial judgment)*

**3. Fix free time at both ends.** The March relief covers transshipment, not your import box in the yard. Negotiate free days at Hamad and at the inland warehouse separately; demurrage starts quickly. *(Industry claim)*

**4. Pre-clear the HS line and the certificate.** The 2026 tariff has been edited on narrow lines twice — the July update and the November poultry change. Have the broker confirm the current rate, and get QGOSM or the public-health certificate before the vessel sails. Neither is available at the gate. See the [certification guide](/guides/certification/). *(Editorial judgment)*

**5. Price the last 40 km.** Hamad is at Umm Al Houl, not Doha. Get a per-container drayage rate for the ring road in peak hours, not a per-shipment estimate. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Transit reality.** Direct China–Hamad sailings are commonly quoted at 18–25 days port-to-port; a relay adds 6–9 days, and clearance adds two to five working days on a clean file. A realistic door-to-door plan for a Doha warehouse is therefore 26–34 days direct and 33–43 days relayed, before any congestion event. Do not publish a 20-day promise on a relay booking. *(Industry claim + editorial judgment)*

## Clearance at Hamad: the file and the free zone

Import clearance runs through Qatar Customs, with a dedicated department for Hamad Port and the southern ports. The declaration needs the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin where a preferential rate is claimed, and the conformity certificate for regulated goods. Duty is assessed at 5% on CIF for most lines. There is no VAT. *(Industry claim)*

The failure modes are consistent:

- **Consignee mismatch.** The B/L consignee must match the importer's commercial registration and customs code. A last-minute change means an amendment, a missed vessel or a storage bill.
- **Certificate gap.** QGOSM covers tyres, brake pads, seat belts, rims and some electricals. It does not cover food, which needs a separate public-health route. "We have CE" addresses neither.
- **Valuation query.** A declared value below the customs benchmark triggers a review. Keep the invoice, payment proof and a market comparison.
- **Free-zone handoff.** Goods entering a Qatar free zone are not duty-paid; the transfer out is a separate declaration. The June 2026 rules formalised this, but the paperwork still has to match the entry declaration.

*(Industry claim)*

The sequence for a Doha import is: pre-classify, pre-certify, file before arrival, inspect if flagged, pay duty, release, truck. Each step has a queue. Importers who clear in two days started the file at booking, not at arrival. *(Editorial judgment)*

## Risk and compliance

**Chokepoint risk.** Qatar has no maritime alternative to Hormuz. The port is modern, deep and under-used; the approach is a single political gate. When the strait closed at the end of February 2026, the effect was schedule loss rather than terminal damage — carriers omitted calls, and volumes took months to recover. Any contract that assumes weekly direct calls needs a diversion clause. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

**Security risk.** Qatar intercepted waves of Iranian missiles and drones in March 2026, and a cargo vessel was struck by a drone in Qatari waters northeast of Mesaieed in May 2026. Hamad Port itself has not been reported as a target, but the country's maritime space has been. War-risk cover must name the port and the inland leg, not just "Gulf." *(Reported fact)*

**Sanctions and re-export.** Cargo transiting Hamad for Iraq or free-zone re-export carries end-use screening obligations. The documentation follows the goods. Screen the counterparty, keep the end-use declaration, and refuse a "free zone, no questions" arrangement. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Cargo abandonment.** An uncleared container accrues storage and can eventually be sold by the port authority. On a relay booking the risk is higher, because the consignee may not be the party who booked the leg. Name a responsible party for clearance in the contract. *(Industry claim)*

## Our read

**Hamad is the best terminal in the Gulf with the least traffic.** On a direct call, the berth, crane and reefer capacity are not your problem, and the March 2026 fee relief makes it a genuinely competitive relay point. The risk is schedule, not infrastructure. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Do not build a DDP price on a relay without a connection guarantee.** The 6–9 day relay penalty is the difference between a good quote and a losing one. If the carrier will not guarantee the connection, price the risk or route via a port with a direct call — see the [direct-versus-landbridge comparison](/compare/jebel-ali-vs-direct/) for how the same problem is handled in Dubai. *(Editorial judgment)*

**Watch the tariff, not the headlines.** The 2026 duty changes have been line-by-line edits. A quarterly recheck of your top ten HS lines is worth more than a monthly rate review. *(Editorial judgment)*

**What would change our mind.** Two consecutive quarters of direct weekly calls from a Chinese base port, a published channel-deepening decision beyond 14 m, and a stable war-risk market. *(Editorial judgment)*

## FAQ

**Is Hamad Port open and operating?**
Yes. It handled more than 59,000 TEU and 249 vessel calls in October 2026. The constraint is service frequency and the Hormuz approach, not terminal operations. *(Reported fact)*

**What is the maximum draft at Hamad Port?**
Fourteen metres in the channel; 17 m alongside at CT1 and CT2. The channel is the limiting number. *(Reported fact)*

**How long does China–Hamad transit take?**
Commonly 18–25 days port-to-port direct and 24–34 days with transshipment. Add two to five working days for clearance and one to three days for drayage. *(Industry claim)*

**Do I pay VAT in Qatar?**
No. You pay 5% customs duty on CIF for most goods, plus product-specific fees. *(Reported fact)*

**Can I use Hamad as a relay to Iraq or Kuwait?**
Yes. The March 2026 relief was designed to encourage it: up to 30 days of free transshipment storage and halved RoRo handling. Confirm the onward booking and the customs status before you commit. *(Reported fact + editorial judgment)*

**What certificates do I need?**
QGOSM conformity for tyres, brake pads, seat belts, rims and some electricals; a Ministry of Public Health certificate for food. The [certification guide](/guides/certification/) lists the categories. *(Industry claim)*
