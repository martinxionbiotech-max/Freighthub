## What changed

Bahrain's VAT rate has been 10% since January 2022, doubled from the original 5%. For importers, the rate itself is not new. What changes the calculation is the interaction with Saudi Arabia's 15% VAT and the King Fahd Causeway.

A shipment that enters Bahrain and then moves to Saudi Arabia can be structured in two ways:

- **Bahrain import, then Saudi export.** Bahrain duty and VAT apply at import; the Saudi sale is an export from Bahrain and an import into Saudi Arabia.
- **Transit through Bahrain.** The goods remain under a transit or free-zone procedure, and Saudi duty and VAT apply at the causeway.

The difference in cash flow and documentation is significant.

## Why it matters

The causeway is not a border between two unrelated markets. It is a short road bridge with customs on both sides, and the cargo can be treated as a Bahrain import or a Saudi import depending on the declaration.

If the cargo is declared as a Bahrain import:

- 5% duty and 10% VAT apply in Bahrain.
- The Saudi buyer then imports from Bahrain, paying Saudi duty and 15% VAT.
- The same goods can bear two VAT charges, with only one recoverable by each party.

If the cargo is declared as transit:

- Bahrain duty and VAT are not paid (subject to the transit rules).
- Saudi duty and 15% VAT apply at the causeway.
- The documentation must show the transit status from the start.

The second structure is usually cheaper, but it requires the paperwork to be right before arrival.

## Who is affected

- Saudi distributors that hold stock in Bahrain.
- Bahraini traders that re-export to Saudi Arabia.
- Free-zone tenants in the Bahrain Logistics Zone.
- Importers using Bahrain as a staging point for the Eastern Province.

## What to do

1. **Decide the status before booking.** Bahrain import or Saudi transit.
2. **Tell the broker and the shipping line.** The bill of lading and the manifest should reflect the intended status.
3. **Check the free-zone rules.** A Bahrain Logistics Zone tenant may hold stock without duty, but the Saudi import still applies on exit.
4. **Confirm the Saudi requirements.** SABER, the Saudi importer and the 15% VAT apply at the causeway.
5. **Keep the two files separate.** A Bahrain import declaration and a Saudi transit declaration are different documents.

## Our read

Bahrain's 10% VAT is not the main issue. The main issue is the double-import risk: a shipment that is declared as a Bahrain import and then sold into Saudi Arabia can pay two VAT charges and two sets of compliance costs.

The fix is structural, not negotiable at the border. Decide the status, document it, and make sure the Saudi importer is ready before the truck reaches the causeway.
