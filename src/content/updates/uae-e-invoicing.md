## What changed

The UAE is introducing electronic invoicing for business-to-business and business-to-government transactions in phases. The rollout is staged by business size and readiness, with the largest businesses in the first wave and smaller businesses later. The Federal Tax Authority is the authority to confirm the exact phase and the technical requirements.

The direction is clear even where the timetable is still being confirmed: invoices will need to be issued in a structured electronic format, validated and reported through an accredited service provider.

## Why it matters

For importers, e-invoicing is not only a finance project. It touches three freight processes:

- **Customs valuation.** The invoice is the base document for the customs declaration. A structured e-invoice must carry the same data — seller, buyer, description, quantity, value, Incoterm, origin — in a form the broker can use.
- **VAT recovery.** The input VAT claim depends on a valid tax invoice. An e-invoice that does not meet the format may not support the claim.
- **Supplier onboarding.** A Chinese supplier that cannot issue a compliant e-invoice may need a local entity or an accredited provider to invoice on its behalf.

## Who is affected

- UAE-registered importers and distributors.
- Free-zone companies that invoice into the mainland.
- Ecommerce sellers with a UAE entity.
- Foreign suppliers with a UAE branch or a local invoicing arrangement.

## What to do

1. **Confirm your phase.** Check the FTA guidance and your turnover.
2. **Map the invoice data.** Compare the current commercial invoice with the customs declaration fields.
3. **Choose a provider.** An accredited e-invoicing service provider, integrated with your ERP.
4. **Test with your broker.** Send a sample e-invoice and confirm the broker can use it for the declaration.
5. **Check the VAT recovery trail.** Make sure the e-invoice supports the input VAT claim.
6. **Brief suppliers.** A supplier that cannot issue a compliant invoice is a supply-chain risk.

## Our read

E-invoicing is a compliance project with a freight consequence. The importers who treat it as a finance-only task discover that the customs broker cannot read the new format, or that the VAT claim is missing a field.

The sensible approach is to test one shipment end to end: e-invoice, customs declaration, VAT return. Fix the gaps before the mandate reaches your business.
