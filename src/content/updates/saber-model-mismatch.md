## What changed

No rule change — the enforcement pattern is what matters. Saudi importers continue to report that the most common SABER failure is not a missing certificate but a **mismatch**: the PCoC covers one model, the shipment contains another. The shipment certificate (SCoC) cannot be issued, and the container sits while the file is corrected.

The pattern is consistent across electricals, appliances, building materials and consumer goods. It is a data problem, not a testing problem.

## Why it matters

A model mismatch is expensive in a way that a missing document is not. The product has already been tested, certified and shipped. The certificate exists. The only problem is that the model number, the rating or the factory on the certificate does not match the invoice and the product label.

The consequences:

- The SCoC cannot be issued.
- Customs cannot release the container.
- Storage and demurrage accrue.
- The buyer may reject the shipment or renegotiate.
- Re-testing or re-certifying can take weeks.

## Who is affected

- Exporters that ship product variants under one certificate.
- Suppliers that change a component, a rating or a factory without updating the certificate.
- Importers that buy from a trading company rather than the manufacturer named on the PCoC.
- Ecommerce sellers that list a new model before the certificate is updated.

## What to do

1. **Match the certificate to the invoice.** Model, rating, factory, brand and HS code.
2. **Check the label.** The product label must match the certificate and the invoice.
3. **Confirm the importer.** The PCoC must be registered to the importer on the shipment.
4. **Freeze the model list.** Do not ship a variant that is not on the certificate.
5. **Update the certificate before the change.** If a component or factory changes, re-certify before the next shipment.
6. **Keep a model matrix.** A simple table of model, certificate number, validity and importer prevents most mismatches.

## Our read

SABER is a data system, and data systems punish inconsistency. The exporters who fail are not the ones who cannot test a product; they are the ones who treat the certificate as a one-time document and then change the product, the factory or the importer without updating it.

The fix is operational, not technical: a model matrix, a pre-shipment check and a rule that no variant ships without a certificate line.
