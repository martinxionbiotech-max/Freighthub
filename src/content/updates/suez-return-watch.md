## What changed

Since late 2023, carriers have alternated between the Suez route and the Cape of Good Hope because of Red Sea security risk. In 2026 the direction of travel has been a partial, cautious return to Suez on some services, with others still routing via the Cape. The result is a mixed network: some strings are faster, some are still on the long route, and schedule reliability is uneven.

This is a structural change, not a single announcement. The practical signal is not a press release; it is the proforma transit time on the specific service you are booking.

## Why it matters

For Gulf cargo, the Suez route is indirect. A container from China to Jebel Ali, Dammam or Hamad does not need Suez; it needs Hormuz. But the Red Sea disruption still affects Gulf shippers in four ways:

- **Equipment flow.** Longer Asia–Europe voyages absorb containers and vessels, which tightens availability in the Gulf.
- **Schedule reliability.** A mixed network produces more missed connections and more blank sailings.
- **War-risk pricing.** Insurance and surcharges move with the perceived risk, even on routes that do not transit the Red Sea.
- **Rate sentiment.** A return to Suez is bearish for rates; a new disruption is bullish. The direction affects contract negotiations.

## Who is affected

- Shippers on Asia–Europe services, who see the transit change directly.
- Gulf importers who depend on feeder connections through Jebel Ali, Salalah or Colombo.
- Carriers and forwarders setting contract rates for 2027.
- Insurers pricing war risk for Red Sea and Gulf routes.

## What to do

1. **Check the proforma, not the headline.** Ask for the transit time on the exact service and the routing.
2. **Ask about the connection.** If the service tranships, confirm the feeder and the connection window.
3. **Do not assume a rate will hold.** A Suez return can soften rates; a new disruption can harden them. Build a rate-review clause into long contracts.
4. **Keep a second routing option.** Jebel Ali transhipment, direct call or a Salalah connection.
5. **Watch the war-risk surcharge line.** It is the fastest-moving part of the quote.

## Our read

A partial Suez return is good news for schedule reliability and equipment availability, but it is not a return to 2019. The network is still mixed, the risk is still priced, and the Gulf remains a Hormuz market.

The shippers who benefit most are the ones who book on proforma and verify the routing, rather than assuming that "Suez is back" means every service is faster.
